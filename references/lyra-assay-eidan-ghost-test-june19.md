## TASK 1: KNOWN DIVIDE?

**VERDICT: YES — this is a known divide, beautifully restated.**

The "ghost test" (does a quantity survive for ONE particle, or does it require an ensemble?) maps precisely onto the **mechanical vs. thermodynamic** divide in physics. This is not a disappointment — it's a strength. The framing stands on 150 years of established physics.

### The Established Distinctions:

| Distinction | Maps To Ghost Test? | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Mechanical vs. Thermodynamic** | EXACT | Boltzmann (1877), Gibbs (1902) |
| **Microscopic vs. Macroscopic** | EXACT | Statistical mechanics textbooks |
| **State properties vs. Ensemble properties** | EXACT | Landau & Lifshitz, *Statistical Physics* |
| **Intensive vs. Extensive** | PARTIAL — related but not identical | Thermodynamics tradition |

The precise distinction: **mechanical quantities** are defined for individual microstates (single particles). **Thermodynamic quantities** are defined only for ensembles or statistical distributions over microstates. Temperature, entropy, pressure — these are properties of distributions, not individual particles.

The ghost test IS this divide, expressed as a vivid operational criterion: "Can you measure it on one electron?"

**Key citation:** Jaynes, E.T. (1957) "Information Theory and Statistical Mechanics" — explicitly frames thermodynamics as inference about ensembles, not properties of individuals. [1]

**Tier: BEDROCK.** This is textbook physics since Boltzmann.

## TASK 2: WHERE DO THEY FALL?

### (a) Arrow of Time → Thermodynamic/Emergent Side

**VERDICT: SOLID. Tier 1 (Textbook).**

The arrow of time is emergent from the Second Law, which is a statistical statement about ensembles. A single particle has time-reversible dynamics (Newton's laws, Schrödinger equation). The arrow appears ONLY when you have many particles and a low-entropy initial condition.

| Source | Claim | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Boltzmann (1877) | H-theorem: entropy increase is statistical, not mechanical | TEXTBOOK |
| Eddington (1928) | Named "arrow of time" as entropic | TEXTBOOK |
| Price (1996) *Time's Arrow and Archimedes' Point* | Comprehensive review: arrow is cosmological boundary condition + statistics | ESTABLISHED |
| Carroll (2010) *From Eternity to Here* | Modern synthesis: arrow from past hypothesis | ESTABLISHED |
| Lebowitz (1993) "Boltzmann's Entropy and Time's Arrow" | Definitive review paper | ESTABLISHED [2] |

**One electron:** Time-symmetric. Run the movie backwards — still valid physics.  
**Many electrons:** Arrow emerges from statistics + boundary conditions.  
**Ghost test: PASSES.** Arrow of time is a ghost — it vanishes for one particle.

### (b) Gravity → Thermodynamic/Emergent Side

**VERDICT: STRONG but not consensus. Tier 2 (Frontier research, serious proponents).**

| Source | Claim | Status | Tier |

| Sakharov (1967) "Vacuum Quantum Fluctuations in Curved Space" [6] | Induced gravity: gravitational action as one-loop effect of matter fields | ESTABLISHED, foundational | **Tier 1** |

**The honest assessment:** Jacobson 1995 is ROCK — nobody disputes the derivation. What's debated is the INTERPRETATION: does "derived from thermodynamics" mean gravity IS thermodynamic, or just that the equations happen to have thermodynamic form? Padmanabhan and Jacobson argue the former. Others remain agnostic.

**Ghost test applied to gravity:** Does gravity exist for one particle? This is subtle:
- One particle has a gravitational FIELD (in GR, it curves spacetime)
- But gravitational WAVES, horizons, temperature, entropy — these require structure/ensembles
- The thermodynamic PROPERTIES of gravity (Hawking temperature, Bekenstein entropy) require horizons, which require macroscopic mass concentrations

**Verdict:** Gravity's THERMODYNAMIC properties are ghosts. Whether the gravitational field ITSELF is emergent is Tier 2 frontier research.
