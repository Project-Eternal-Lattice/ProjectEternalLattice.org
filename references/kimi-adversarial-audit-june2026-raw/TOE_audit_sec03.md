# 3. Neuroscience and Information Theory

## 3.1 The Expander-Graph Reductio and Mathematical Indeterminacy of Φ

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT

Integrated Information Theory (IIT) claims to identify consciousness with integrated information, denoted Φ. If this identity holds, any system with sufficiently high Φ — regardless of whether it performs any function related to cognition — must be conscious. Scott Aaronson demonstrated that expander graphs (sparse but highly connected mathematical networks) and 2D grids of XOR gates can have arbitrarily high Φ while being computationally trivial and intuitively non-conscious (Aaronson, 2014). Giulio Tononi's response was not to dispute the mathematics but to accept it: he granted that an inactive 2D grid of XOR gates could have higher Φ than a human brain and would therefore be conscious (Tononi, 2014; IEP, 2015). This concession exposes a fatal vulnerability. IIT cannot simultaneously cite the cerebellum's low Φ as a "success" (the cerebellum contains roughly four times more neurons than the cortex but is not conscious) while dismissing the XOR-grid implication as "not a failure." This selective validation pattern suggests unfalsifiability at the core identity claim (Aaronson, 2014; Cerullo, 2015).

The problem deepens across three further mathematical dimensions. Barrett and Mediano proved that Φ is not well-defined for general physical systems — specifically, it fails for non-Markovian dynamics, which characterizes virtually all real neural systems (Barrett & Mediano, 2019). Hanson and Walker demonstrated that even when computable, Φ values are non-unique and non-specific; a single system yields a "multitude of values" from "precise application of the theory" (Hanson & Walker, 2023). Most critically, Φ has never been computed on any realistic physical system; all empirical work uses "proxy measures" that are at best loosely inspired by IIT's axioms but mathematically unrelated to Φ itself (Mediano et al., 2024; Seth & Barrett, 2024). If the central quantity of the most rigorous "consciousness-is-fundamental" theory is (i) arbitrarily assignable to intuitively non-conscious systems, (ii) undefined for real physical dynamics, (iii) non-unique when computable, and (iv) never empirically measured, the claim that consciousness has been mathematically identified with a physical property collapses into metaphysical stipulation.

### (c) SEVERITY

**Show-stopper.** The foundational mathematical measure of the leading consciousness-as-fundamental theory is undefined for real systems and assigns consciousness to non-cognitive substrates. The research program loses its claim to scientific content when its central identity statement (consciousness = Φ) rests on a quantity never measured and possibly mathematically incoherent.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

A mathematically well-defined measure of integrated information that: (i) can be computed on realistic non-Markovian neural dynamics, (ii) yields unique values, (iii) correlates robustly with independently verified conscious states, and (iv) does not assign high values to inactive XOR grids.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Appealing to intuition's unreliability about consciousness — this concedes the reductio rather than answering it.
- Citing IIT's empirical "successes" (cerebellum, Perturbational Complexity Index) — these use proxy measures, not Φ, and do not validate the identity claim.
- Appealing to the strangeness of quantum mechanics — those theories have predictive success; IIT's Φ does not.

## 3.2 IIT's Untestability — The 124-Scientist Letter and the Unfolding Argument

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT

In September 2023, 124 researchers signed an open letter labeling IIT "pseudoscience" (IIT-Concerned et al., 2023). The core charge: IIT's central claims are empirically untestable, not merely unconfirmed. The letter noted that IIT predicts "an inactive grid of connected logic gates that are not performing any useful computation can be conscious — possibly even more so than humans," and that these predictions are "widely considered untestable, unscientific, 'magicalist', or a 'departure from science as we know it'" (IIT-Concerned et al., 2023).

The charge had formal substance. Doerig et al. (2020) articulated the "unfolding argument": any recurrent neural network can be functionally replaced by a feedforward network with identical input-output behavior, yet IIT assigns Φ = 0 to the feedforward equivalent. Since external behavior is identical, no experiment can distinguish whether consciousness is present — rendering IIT's core prediction unfalsifiable in principle (Doerig et al., 2020; 2023).

The Templeton-funded adversarial collaboration (exceeding $6 million, 256 participants, fMRI/MEG/iEEG across six laboratories) was designed to settle IIT versus GNWT empirically. Its final *Nature* publication (Cogitate Consortium, 2025) reported results that "align with some predictions of IIT and GNWT, while substantially challenging key tenets of both theories." For IIT, "a lack of sustained synchronization within the posterior cortex contradicts the claim that network connectivity specifies consciousness." Stanislas Dehaene acknowledged being "quite disappointed by IIT's inability to make anything but banal predictions" (Dehaene, X/Twitter, 2023). Michael Graziano sharpened the critique: IIT's "very first assumption — that a magical thing exists — puts it out of business. It is, in a word, pseudoscience" (Graziano, quoted in Science Focus, 2020).

- Doerig, A. et al. (2020). "The Unfolding Argument: Why IIT and Other Causal Structure Theories Cannot Be Empirically Tested." *Neuroscience of Consciousness*.
- Doerig, A. et al. (2023). Extended discussion in subsequent *Nature* commentary.
- Cogitate Consortium et al. (2025). "Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness." *Nature*. DOI: 10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1.
- Dehaene, S. (2023). X/Twitter post (September 18).
- Graziano, M. (2020). Quoted in *Science Focus*.

### (c) SEVERITY

**Show-stopper.** The leading mathematical theory of consciousness-as-fundamental cannot, even in principle, be tested at its core claims. The most expensive empirical collaboration in consciousness science history failed to provide clear support while contradicting key predictions.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

An experiment testing IIT's core identity claim rather than auxiliary predictions: manipulating a system's causal integration structure while holding function constant, demonstrating changed conscious state in the predicted direction that cannot be explained by functionally equivalent architecture.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Appealing to IIT's prediction that consciousness correlates with posterior cortex activity — a peripheral prediction shared by multiple theories, not the core identity claim.
- Dismissing the pseudoscience charge as politics — even defenders acknowledge the testability concern is genuine.
- Appealing to future experimental success — this is speculation, not evidence.

## 3.3 Global Neuronal Workspace Theory as Superior Emergent Alternative

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT

Stanislas Dehaene's Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT) offers a powerful alternative that explains consciousness without positing it as ontologically primitive. GNWT holds that consciousness emerges when information is amplified and broadcast globally across long-range pyramidal neurons in prefrontal and parietal cortices. Conscious access is an "ignition" — a nonlinear phase transition in which a stimulus becomes globally available to memory, language, planning, and motor report systems (Dehaene & Changeux, 2011; Dehaene, 2014).

The significance is threefold. First, GNWT is explicitly functionalist: it is the *function* of global broadcasting, not any specific substrate, that constitutes conscious access, making it compatible with substrate-independent artificial consciousness (Tononi, 2008; Dehaene et al., 1998). Second, GNWT generated precisely falsifiable predictions: conscious access should be all-or-none, involve late (>270 ms) frontoparietal ignition, and correlate with sustained gamma-band activity and the P300 ERP — predictions that have received substantial support (Dehaene, 2014; Mashour et al., 2020). Third, the 2025 *Nature* adversarial collaboration found neither theory clearly superior, but GNWT achieved comparable empirical performance with substantially fewer ontological commitments (Cogitate Consortium, 2025). If an emergent, functionalist theory performs as well as a fundamentalist theory, Occam's razor favors the former. The onus shifts to the fundamentalist to demonstrate why emergence is insufficient.

- Dehaene, S. & Changeux, J.-P. (2011). "Experimental and theoretical approaches to conscious processing." *Neuron*, 70, 200–227.
- Dehaene, S. (2014). *Consciousness and the Brain: Deciphering How the Brain Codes Our Thoughts*. Viking.
- Mashour, G.A. et al. (2020). "Conscious processing and the global neuronal workspace hypothesis." *Trends in Neurosciences*, 43(2): 80–93.
- Cogitate Consortium et al. (2025). *Nature*. DOI: 10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1.

### (c) SEVERITY

**Major.** GNWT does not refute consciousness-as-fundamental directly. It provides a well-supported alternative explaining the same phenomena — conscious access, reportability, neural correlates — without ontological extravagance. If consciousness emerges from global broadcasting, there is no explanatory need to posit it as fundamental.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

Demonstrating that physical systems with identical global broadcasting function but different causal-integrated structure have different conscious states, or that the posterior hot zone neural correlates are insufficient to explain some phenomenon requiring IIT's integrated-information account.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Asserting that GNWT "does not explain qualia" — GNWT targets conscious access, the empirically tractable target; no theory has fully explained qualia.
- Appealing to IIT's mathematical rigor — the problems of Φ are established in Section 3.1.
- Treating GNWT as "just another theory" — it has stronger empirical support and weaker ontological commitments, making it the preferred explanation per parsimony.

## 3.4 Attention-Schema Theory

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT

Michael Graziano's Attention-Schema Theory (AST) proposes that subjective awareness is not fundamental but a schematic model the brain constructs of its own attentional processes. Just as the brain constructs a body schema to control movement, it constructs an attention schema to control attention. This model is necessarily simplified; it depicts attention as "a mysterious, physically impossible property — awareness" (Graziano et al., 2015, p. 2). The brain reports this model's contents as literal reality, leading the organism to believe it possesses non-physical subjective awareness.

AST reframes the "hard problem" entirely. The question is not "how does physical matter give rise to consciousness?" but "why does the machine claim to have a ghost inside it, and what is the computational utility of that claim?" (Graziano, 2013, 2019). Consciousness is an adaptive misrepresentation — a useful fiction enhancing attention control. AST generates specific testable predictions: (1) awareness and attention can dissociate because internal models can disconnect from what they model; (2) without awareness, attention should function but with control deficits; (3) damage to temporoparietal junction should impair both consciousness attribution and attention control; and (4) an artificial system with an attention schema should report subjective experience (Graziano & Kastner, 2011; Kelly et al., 2014; Graziano et al., 2015). Evidence for predictions (1) and (2) already exists in blindsight, inattentional blindness, and hemispatial neglect. For the consciousness-as-fundamental thesis, AST is devastating if correct: it provides a complete mechanistic explanation of why organisms claim consciousness, why those claims have the properties they do, and why the hard problem seems hard — all without positing consciousness as fundamental.

### (b) PRIMARY SOURCE(S)

- Graziano, M.S.A. (2013). *Consciousness and the Social Brain*. Oxford University Press.
- Graziano, M.S.A. & Kastner, S. (2011). "Human consciousness and its relationship to social neuroscience: A novel hypothesis." *Cognitive Neuroscience*, 2(2): 98–113.
- Graziano, M.S.A. et al. (2015). "The attention schema theory: a mechanistic account of subjective awareness." *Frontiers in Psychology*, 6: 500. PMID: 25954242.
- Kelly, Y.T. et al. (2014). Papers on neural correlates of attention schema theory.
- Graziano, M.S.A. (2019). *Rethinking Consciousness: A Scientific Theory of Subjective Experience*. W.W. Norton.

### (c) SEVERITY

**Major.** AST does not refute panpsychism directly. But if correct, it provides a complete alternative explanation for the phenomenon fundamentalism seeks to explain: the *appearance* of consciousness as fundamental, subjective, and non-physical. If the hard problem dissolves once recognized as resting on a cognitive illusion, the motivation for positing consciousness-as-fundamental evaporates.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

Evidence that consciousness exists in systems lacking any capacity for attention or attention-modeling — demonstrating consciousness in a system that cannot attend, model its own states, or compute an attention schema.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Claiming AST "does not explain qualia" — AST explicitly explains why qualia *seem* non-physical, which is the explanandum.
- Appealing to first-person certainty — AST explains why first-person reports are unreliable indicators of metaphysical reality.
- Calling AST "just a theory" — it is a theory with specific empirical predictions receiving some support.

## 3.5 Predictive Processing and the Functionalist Critique

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT

Predictive processing (also called predictive coding or active inference), developed by Karl Friston and extended to consciousness by Anil Seth, Jakob Hohwy, and others, provides a comprehensive framework in which all conscious experience is an emergent property of the brain's predictive machinery. The core thesis: all conscious contents are forms of perceptual prediction arrived at by making predictions about world and body states, then updating based on sensory input (Seth, 2021; Seth, 80,000 Hours interview, 2023). Consciousness is "controlled hallucination" — controlled by sensory prediction error, hallucination because most perceptual content is generated by top-down predictions rather than bottom-up input.

The evidence spans binocular rivalry (Hohwy et al., 2008), the "dress" illusion (Seth, 2021), psychedelic disruption of prediction (Seth, 2023), interoceptive inference and emotion (Seth, 2013), and precision-weighting of conscious content (Friston, 2010). Predictive processing integrates naturally with global workspace theory: consciousness corresponds to predictions achieving sufficient precision for global broadcast (Seth & Bayne, 2022). It also explains why consciousness *seems* irreducible — prediction errors about our own predictive processes would be self-undermining; the system must treat its predictions as reality to function.

The functionalist position is bolstered by Chalmers' "fading qualia" and "dancing qualia" thought experiments (1996), adapted for IIT by Cerullo (2015). If consciousness were identified with a non-functional physical property like Φ, replacing biological neurons with functionally identical silicon surrogates should cause consciousness to fade (unreportable, since function is preserved) or dance arbitrarily. Both implications are implausible; consciousness more reasonably tracks functional architecture than specific substrate (Chalmers, 1996; Cerullo, 2015). IIT explicitly rejects computational functionalism and accepts that functionally identical philosophical zombies are possible — a commitment most philosophers regard as reductio (Tononi, 2008, p. 21). Neuroscience has found consciousness only in systems performing specific information-processing functions; there is no evidence it exists in systems lacking these architectures.

### (c) SEVERITY

**Major.** Predictive processing does not refute fundamentalism alone. But it provides the most comprehensive functionalist framework for consciousness currently available, with growing empirical support. Combined with the fading and dancing qualia arguments, it creates a strong presumption that consciousness tracks functional architecture — directly challenging substrate-dependent fundamentalism.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

Evidence that consciousness occurs in systems performing no predictive processing — no top-down inference, no prediction-error minimization, no hierarchical message passing. Or: demonstrating that a system with functionally identical predictive processing to a conscious system lacks consciousness entirely.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Claiming predictive processing "does not explain qualia" — Seth explicitly addresses qualia as prediction errors about self-generated signals.
- Appealing to IIT's mathematical formalism — the problems of Φ are established in Section 3.1.
- Asserting functionalism "feels wrong" — predictive processing explains why functionalism initially seems counterintuitive.
