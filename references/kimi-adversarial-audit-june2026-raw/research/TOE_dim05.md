## Counter-Argument 1: Popperian Demarcation Failure -- Consciousness-as-Fundamental Generates No Risky, Testable Predictions

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT (235 words)

Karl Popper's central contribution to philosophy of science is the criterion of demarcation: a theory is scientific if and only if it is falsifiable -- that is, it generates "potential falsifiers," observable states of affairs that would, if found, refute the theory (Popper, 1935/1959, Ch. 4; Popper, 1963, p. 37). The mark of genuine science is not verifiability (confirmation is trivially easy to obtain) but vulnerability to refutation. Einstein's general relativity was exemplary because it made precise, risky predictions -- e.g., light bending near the sun by a specific, calculable amount -- that could have been empirically disconfirmed (Popper, 1974, pp. 980-981).

The claim that "consciousness is the fundamental substrate of reality" fails this criterion. It does not generate any potential falsifiers -- no observable state of affairs is ruled out by the claim. A universe in which consciousness is fundamental looks, in all observable respects, identical to a materialist universe (Tononi & Koch, 2015): the same laws of physics, the same spatiotemporal structure, the same number of elementary particles, the same causal history. As one analysis puts it, "a panpsychic universe would look indistinguishable from a physical universe" (The Electric Agora, 2015). The claim is compatible with all possible observations, which by Popper's lights makes it not a scientific theory at all but a metaphysical claim -- specifically, what Popper termed a "metaphysical research programme" (Popper, 1974a, pp. 133-143): a framework that may guide research but does not itself make falsifiable predictions. Popper treated such programmes as heuristically valuable but methodologically second-class (Popper, 1982b, p. 165). The consciousness-as-fundamental thesis is not falsifiable even in principle because the conscious aspect is defined as intrinsically private and unobservable from the third-person perspective -- precisely the feature that makes it allegedly "fundamental" also makes it immune to empirical test.

### (b) PRIMARY SOURCE(S)

- Popper, K. (1935/1959). *The Logic of Scientific Discovery*. London: Hutchinson. Ch. 4 ("Falsifiability"), pp. 78-92; Ch. 6 ("Degrees of Testability"), pp. 113-130.
- Popper, K. (1963). *Conjectures and Refutations: The Growth of Scientific Knowledge*. London: Routledge. "Science: Conjectures and Refutations," pp. 33-65; "Replies to My Critics," pp. 961-996.
- Popper, K. (1974a). "Intellectual Autobiography." In P.A. Schilpp (ed.), *The Philosophy of Karl Popper*, pp. 3-181. La Salle, IL: Open Court. [Metaphysical research programme concept, pp. 133-143.]
- Popper, K. (1982b). *Quantum Theory and the Schism in Physics*. London: Hutchinson. [On metaphysical research programmes as "indispensable for science," p. 165.]
- Tononi, G. & Koch, C. (2015). "Consciousness: Here, There and Everywhere?" *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*, 370(1668). ["Besides claiming that matter and mind are one thing, [panpsychism] has little constructive to say and offers no positive laws explaining how the mind is organized and works."]

### (c) SEVERITY

**Show-stopper.** If the Popperian demarcation criterion is accepted, consciousness-as-fundamental is not a scientific theory at all but a metaphysical claim, placing it outside the scope of empirical adjudication and rendering any "scientific" pretensions of the framework illegitimate.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

Generate a *specific, quantitative prediction* that follows from consciousness-being-fundamental but not from materialism -- e.g., a measurable deviation from standard quantum mechanical probabilities at the mesoscale that is predicted by a consciousness-causes-collapse mechanism and calculable in advance (not post-hoc accommodated). Or: identify a physical system whose observable behavior *must* differ depending on whether consciousness is fundamental, with the difference being empirically detectable and the prediction being made *before* observation.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Claiming the framework is "beyond" falsification or operates at a "higher level" than science.
- Appealing to confirmation/verifiability ("it explains consciousness better") rather than risky prediction.
- Saying materialism is also unfalsifiable (the burden of proof falls on the proponent of the novel ontological posit).
- Offering post-hoc accommodations of existing data as if they were predictions.

## Counter-Argument 2: Lakatosian Degenerating Research Programme -- No Novel Confirmed Predictions

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT (248 words)

Imre Lakatos improved upon Popper by evaluating not individual theories but "research programmes" -- sequences of theories sharing a "hard core" of non-negotiable assumptions, surrounded by a "protective belt" of auxiliary hypotheses that are modified to absorb anomalies (Lakatos, 1970, FMSRP: 33-34). A programme is *progressive* if each new theory in the sequence (a) has excess empirical content over its predecessor (theoretically progressive), and (b) some of that novel content is corroborated (empirically progressive). A programme is *degenerating* if it fails on both counts -- if modifications are driven solely by the need to defend the hard core rather than by novel prediction (Lakatos, 1970, FMSRP: 34). For Lakatos, Marxism and Freudian psychoanalysis were paradigmatic degenerating programmes because they accommodated all observations post-hoc; Newtonian mechanics and Einsteinian relativity were progressive because they made and confirmed novel predictions (Lakatos, 1970; Chalmers, 1994, p. 84).

Consciousness-as-fundamental, understood as a research programme, exhibits the hallmarks of degeneration. Its hard core -- that consciousness is a fundamental and ubiquitous feature of physical reality, present even in elementary particles (Goff, 2019, pp. 18-20) -- has not generated any novel, confirmed empirical predictions. Instead, the programme has spent its theoretical resources on defensive manoeuvres: the "combination problem" (how micro-experiences combine into macro-experience) was not predicted but emerged as a crisis requiring accommodation (Chalmers, 2016a; Seager, 1995); the "unfolding argument" showed that leading panpsychist-friendly theories like IIT are pre-falsified (Doerig et al., 2019; Kleiner & Hoel, 2021); and the empirical literature has produced no phenomenon whose discovery was *guided by* the consciousness-as-fundamental hypothesis (Tononi & Koch, 2015). The programme's development has occurred "in response to external criticism" rather than "due to its own internal success" (Larvor, 2006, p. 713). As Lakatos emphasized, even a programme lagging badly behind may stage a comeback -- but the scores must be recorded and publicly displayed (Lakatos, 1971a, p. 101). On the current scoreboard, consciousness-as-fundamental has zero corroborated novel predictions.

- Chalmers, A.F. (1994). *What Is This Thing Called Science?* 3rd ed. Indianapolis: Hackett. [p. 84 on progressive vs. degenerative programmes.]

### (c) SEVERITY

**Major.** A Lakatosian assessment does not banish consciousness-as-fundamental from rational discourse entirely (Lakatos was more permissive than Popper), but it classifies the programme as currently degenerating -- scientifically suspect and intellectually risky to pursue. The severity is "Major" rather than "Show-stopper" because Lakatos allowed that degenerating programmes may recover.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

Produce at least one novel prediction that (i) is entailed by the hard core of consciousness-as-fundamental plus its positive heuristic, (ii) was not already known or accommodated, (iii) is sufficiently specific to risk disconfirmation, and (iv) has been subsequently corroborated by observation or experiment. For example: predicting, in advance, a specific pattern of quantum measurement statistics that differs between conscious and non-conscious measuring devices, and then observing that predicted difference.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Listing post-hoc explanations of known phenomena (e.g., "it explains why we have subjective experience") as if they were novel predictions.
- Claiming that philosophical sophistication or internal coherence counts as "theoretical progress" in the Lakatosian sense -- Lakatos required excess *empirical* content.
- Asserting that the programme is "young" and needs more time -- Lakatos evaluated programmes on their current track record, not their promissory notes.
- Comparing the programme to Einstein's relativity (which made specific, risky predictions within years of its formulation).

## Counter-Argument 3: Unfalsifiability In Principle -- Observational Equivalence with Materialism

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT (243 words)

There is a principled distinction between theories that are "not yet operationalized" (they could in principle be tested once measurement techniques improve) and theories that are "unfalsifiable in principle" (no possible observation could ever discriminate between them and their rivals). Consciousness-as-fundamental falls into the latter category because it is observationally equivalent to materialism/physicalism in all empirically accessible respects (The Electric Agora, 2015; Coyne, cited in Shah, 2025).

The argument proceeds in three steps. First, if consciousness is fundamental and ubiquitous -- present even in electrons and quarks -- but does not alter the publicly observable behavior of those particles (if it did, we would have detected anomalies in physics experiments long ago), then the physical laws governing particle behavior are identical whether or not consciousness is present (Shah, 2025; Chalmers, 1996, p. 305). Second, since all scientific measurement is ultimately of physical behavior, there is no empirical access to the supposed conscious aspect of reality. Third, therefore, a panpsychist universe and a materialist universe are observationally indistinguishable -- scientists in either universe would arrive at the same physical laws, the same experimental results, and the same scientific theories (The Electric Agora, 2015).

This is not a temporary limitation. It is structural: the conscious aspect is defined as private, first-person, and non-physical in the sense of not affecting third-person measurable behavior. As Chalmers himself notes, "there is no direct evidence" for panpsychism, but neither is there "direct evidence against it" -- the claim is precisely positioned to evade empirical contact (Chalmers, 1996, p. 305; Chalmers, in Shah, 2025). John Searle put the point more sharply: panpsychism "does not get up to the level of being false. It is strictly speaking meaningless because no clear notion has been given to the claim" (Searle, cited in Wikipedia/Panpsychism, "Arguments against"). A claim that cannot in principle be distinguished from its negation is not an empirical hypothesis -- it is, in Popper's term, "irrefutability" which is "not a virtue of a theory but a vice" (Popper, 1963, p. 37).

### (b) PRIMARY SOURCE(S)

- Searle, J.R. Cited in: "Panpsychism" -- *Wikipedia*, section "Arguments against/Theoretical issues." ["It does not get up to the level of being false. It is strictly speaking meaningless because no clear notion has been given to the claim."]
- Popper, K. (1963). *Conjectures and Refutations*, p. 37. ["Irrefutability is not a virtue of a theory (as people often think) but a vice."]
- The Electric Agora (2015). "Panpsychism -- Is It Testable?" September 28, 2015. [Arguments (1), (2), and (3) for observational equivalence.]
- Shah, Z.H. (2025). "Critique of Panpsychism: Philosophical Coherence and Scientific Viability." *The Muslim Times*, May 7, 2025. [Cites Seth, Coyne on untestability.]
- Coyne, J. Cited in Shah (2025). ["The claim that inanimate objects like electrons, rocks, and spoons have a form of consciousness is untestable in any way, and so the theory is a non-explanation."]

### (c) SEVERITY

**Show-stopper.** If the thesis is unfalsifiable in principle, it is not an empirical claim at all and cannot be evaluated by scientific methodology. This directly undermines any manuscript presenting consciousness-as-fundamental as a scientific or scientifically-grounded theory.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

Identify a *necessary observable consequence* of consciousness-being-fundamental that materialism/physicalism would *not* predict -- and show that this consequence is empirically detectable in principle (even if not yet detected in practice). The consequence must be such that its absence would count against the thesis. For example: demonstrating that systems with identical physical structure but different conscious states (e.g., a biological brain vs. a functionally identical silicon simulation) *must* exhibit measurably different physical behavior, and specifying in advance what that difference is.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Claiming that future technology will enable testing (this concedes unfalsifiability in the present without addressing the principled barrier).
- Appealing to first-person experience as "evidence" (this begs the question against materialism, which accounts for first-person reports as physical processes).
- Arguing that materialism is also unfalsifiable (this is an tu quoque fallacy; materialism has a track record of risky predictions that consciousness-as-fundamental lacks).
- Pointing to IIT or other proxy measures as evidence (IIT has been shown to be pre-falsified by the unfolding argument; see Doerig et al., 2019).

## Counter-Argument 4: The Operationalization and Measurement Problem -- Circularity via the Unfolding Argument and Kleiner-Hoel Dilemma

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT (250 words)

Even if consciousness is fundamental, a scientific theory must specify how it can be measured independently of the behavioral or neural correlates it is supposed to explain. Doerig, Schurger & Herzog (2020) propose "hard criteria for empirical theories of consciousness," of which the most damning is the requirement that a theory must generate falsifiable predictions that could, in principle, be empirically disconfirmed. But a deeper problem emerges from the "unfolding argument" (Doerig et al., 2019) and its formalization by Kleiner & Hoel (2021): for any theory that assigns consciousness based on a system's internal causal structure, that structure can be "unfolded" into a functionally equivalent feedforward system with the same input-output behavior but zero consciousness, according to the theory's own criteria. Since the two systems are behaviorally indistinguishable, any inference procedure based on behavior (reports, measurements) cannot discriminate between them -- yet the theory predicts different conscious states. This creates a systematic mismatch between predictions and inferences, pre-falsifying the theory (Kleiner & Hoel, 2021, "Falsification and Consciousness").

Kleiner (2020, 2024) extended this to a "no-go theorem" showing that any non-trivial theory of consciousness that relies on physical properties beyond input-output function is subject to a priori falsification. The dilemma (the "Kleiner-Hoel dilemma") has two horns: either the theory's predictions vary under substitutions that preserve the inference data (making it a priori false), or they do not vary (making the theory trivial, since its predictions are strictly determined by the inference data and add no independent content) (Kleiner, 2024; Negro, 2024). The consciousness-as-fundamental thesis faces a particularly acute form of this problem: if consciousness is defined as independent of physical structure (as "fundamental" implies), then there is no principled way to measure it independently of the very physical correlates it is supposed to transcend. The measurement becomes circular -- we infer consciousness from physical behavior, then claim consciousness is fundamental and explains the behavior. As B.F. Skinner observed about vitalism: "Such 'mental way stations' amount to excess theoretical baggage which fails to advance cause-and-effect explanations by substituting an unfathomable psychology of 'mind'" (Skinner, cited in Keating, 2002). The unfolding argument generalizes this critique: any theory that posits consciousness in a system's intrinsic structure while inferring it from behavior is either false or trivial.

- Doerig, A., Schurger, A., et al. (2019). "The Unfolding Argument: Why IIT and Other Causal Structure Theories Cannot Explain Consciousness." *Consciousness and Cognition*, 72, 49-59.
- Kleiner, J. & Hoel, E. (2021). "Falsification and Consciousness." *Neuroscience of Consciousness*, 2021(1), niab001.

- Skinner, B.F. Cited in Keating (2002).

### (c) SEVERITY

**Major.** The measurement circularity does not refute consciousness-as-fundamental as a philosophical position, but it blocks the path from philosophical claim to empirical science. If the thesis cannot be operationalized without circularity, it cannot function as the basis for a scientific research programme.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

Develop an independent measurement procedure for consciousness that does not rely on behavioral or neural correlates -- a "consciousness detector" that measures the fundamental property directly, analogously to how a voltmeter measures electric potential without inferring it from the behavior of charged particles. Alternatively: reformulate the thesis so that it makes predictions solely about *physical* observables that are sensitive to the presence/absence of consciousness, while providing a principled argument for why those observables are trustworthy indicators (not mere correlates).

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Claiming that introspection or first-person report is the measurement (this is the very correlate that needs independent validation).
- Appealing to IIT's Phi measure (pre-falsified by the unfolding argument; computationally intractable; correlates rather than measures).
- Arguing that no theory of consciousness can escape the dilemma (this would be an admission of skepticism about all consciousness science, not a defense of this specific thesis).
- Saying consciousness is "directly known" and therefore needs no independent measurement (this concedes the circularity and abandons empirical methodology).

## Counter-Argument 5: The Vitalism/Aether Analogy -- Historical Precedent of "Fundamental" Unobservables

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT (247 words)

History of science offers a sobering pattern: theories that posit a fundamental, unobservable entity or force to explain a mysterious phenomenon tend to be abandoned when (a) they fail to generate testable predictions, and (b) a rival framework explains the same phenomena without the extra posit. Vitalism posited an "elan vital" -- a non-physical life force present in all living things (Bergson, 1911; Driesch, 1914). Luminiferous aether posited a universal medium for light propagation. Both were defended as metaphysically necessary, intuitively compelling, and required by the phenomena. Both were ultimately discarded not because they were positively falsified (they were too flexible for that) but because they were rendered superfluous by progressive research programmes that explained the same phenomena with fewer ontological commitments: molecular biology and organic chemistry for vitalism (Wohler's synthesis of urea in 1828 showed organic compounds could be created without a vital force; see Gupta, 2000); Maxwell's electromagnetic field equations for aether (which showed light propagation required no medium). As Gupta (2000) notes, vitalism "stood discredited" when Wohler synthesized urea in vitro, negating Berzelius's claim that organic compounds required a vital force. Sokal (cited in Wikipedia/Vitalism) concluded: "Mainstream science has rejected vitalism since at least the 1930s, for a plethora of good reasons that have only become stronger with time." Keating (2002) observed that vitalism's "circular reasoning offers pseudo-explanation, and may deceive us into believing we have explained some aspect of biology when in fact we have only labeled our ignorance."

Consciousness-as-fundamental fits this pattern precisely. It posits a fundamental, unobservable property (consciousness in elementary particles) to explain a mysterious phenomenon (subjective experience) that a rival framework (emergentist physicalism) is increasingly accounting for through mechanistic neuroscience: neural correlates of consciousness, predictive processing, global workspace theory, and integrated information measures (even if imperfect) all generate testable predictions about which systems are conscious and to what degree (Doerig et al., 2020; Seth, 2021). Keith Frankish draws the analogy explicitly: consciousness-as-mysterious may follow the same trajectory as vitalism -- once we explain the functions, the "special ingredient" evaporates (Frankish, podcast interview, 2025). Anil Seth makes the same comparison: the hard problem of consciousness may "dissipate into the ether as vitalism did in the past, not by some single discovery that disproves any claims to the metaphysical significance of consciousness, but simply by the gradual accumulation of scientific understanding" (Seth, cited in Atlas Writes, 2025). The track record of "fundamental unobservable" posits in science is poor. The burden of proof lies squarely on consciousness-as-fundamental to demonstrate why it will not follow the same trajectory.

### (c) SEVERITY

**Major.** The historical analogy does not logically entail that consciousness-as-fundamental is false, but it places a heavy burden of proof on the thesis. The track record of "fundamental unobservable" explanations in science is one of progressive elimination as rival frameworks succeed without them. The severity is "Major" because it provides strong inductive grounds for skepticism, but not "Show-stopper" because the future may always differ from the past.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

Demonstrate a *disanalogy* with vitalism/aether by showing that consciousness-as-fundamental (a) has made at least one novel, confirmed prediction that vitalism/aether never achieved, and (b) explains something that emergentist physicalism *cannot* explain even in principle -- not merely something it has not *yet* explained, but something that is structurally beyond its explanatory resources. Alternatively: show that the "combination problem" has a principled solution that does not merely recreate the hard problem at a different level, which would differentiate panpsychism from the "just-so stories" of vitalism.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Claiming that vitalism was "different" without specifying the relevant difference (special pleading).
- Appealing to the authority of historical figures (Planck, Schrodinger, Eddington) who endorsed consciousness-as-fundamental (they did so without testable predictions, which is precisely the problem).
- Saying physicalism "hasn't solved consciousness yet" (this concedes that the comparison is apt and the jury is still out, which favors the skeptical position).
- Arguing that vitalism's failure doesn't prove consciousness-as-fundamental will fail (true, but absence of disanalogy sustains the inductive concern).
- Claiming the hard problem is intrinsically different from the "life problem" (must be demonstrated, not asserted; the parallel in explanatory structure is what drives the analogy).

## Meta-Observation: Interaction of Objections

These five counter-arguments reinforce each other. Counter-Argument 1 (Popperian) establishes that the thesis fails the minimum threshold for scientific status. Counter-Argument 3 (unfalsifiability in principle) explains *why* it fails: observational equivalence makes empirical discrimination impossible. Counter-Argument 2 (Lakatosian) shows that even if granted provisional scientific status, the programme is degenerating. Counter-Argument 4 (measurement circularity) identifies the structural mechanism behind the unfalsifiability. Counter-Argument 5 (vitalism/aether) provides the historical precedent suggesting the pattern is not accidental.

Taken together, they present a methodological case that consciousness-as-fundamental is not (currently) a scientific theory and shows no signs of becoming one. It may be a valid metaphysical position, but the manuscript must not conflate metaphysical validity with scientific status.

## Flagged Uncertainties

1. **Strength of Popperian critique**: Some philosophers (e.g., Kuhn, Feyerabend) have argued that Popper's demarcation criterion is too strict and would exclude much of what practicing scientists regard as legitimate science. If Popper's criterion is rejected or weakened, Counter-Argument 1 loses some but not all force -- the thesis would still need to meet whatever demarcation standard replaces Popper's. I am confident the Popperian critique is strong but not confident it is decisive against all possible reformulations.

2. **Lakatosian assessment timing**: Lakatos emphasized that assessments are time-indexed and contextual. A programme that is degenerating today may recover tomorrow. I am confident the *current* assessment is "degenerating" but not confident this will remain true indefinitely.

3. **Vitalism disanalogy**: Proponents of consciousness-as-fundamental may argue that consciousness is *directly known* in a way vital force was not (we experience our own consciousness, but never experienced elan vital directly). This is a prima facie disanalogy that should be engaged seriously. I am not confident I have the strongest possible response to this disanalogy in this document.

4. **IIT's status**: The 2023 Templeton-funded adversarial collaboration gave IIT a "slight edge" over Global Neuronal Workspace Theory, but results were inconclusive (IIT-Concerned, 2023). Some commentators argue this provides partial empirical vindication for panpsychism-friendly theories. However, the unfolding argument's logical force is independent of IIT's empirical performance. I flag this as an area of active dispute.

5. **"Not yet operationalized" vs. "unfalsifiable in principle"**: The strongest possible defense of consciousness-as-fundamental would argue that the thesis *could* generate testable predictions if sufficiently developed -- e.g., via a "post-Galilean" science that incorporates first-person data (Goff, 2019, ch. 8). I acknowledge this as a live possibility but note it is currently a promissory note with no fulfilled promises.
