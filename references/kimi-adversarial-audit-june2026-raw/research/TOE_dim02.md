# Domain 2: Physics & Quantum Foundations -- Counter-Arguments

## Scope

This document presents the 5 strongest academic counter-arguments in physics and quantum foundations against the thesis that "consciousness is the fundamental substrate of reality -- not emergent from physical processes." These objections are drawn from mainstream quantum mechanics, decoherence theory, alternative interpretations (Bohmian mechanics, QBism, relational QM), objective collapse models, and quantum information theory. Each counter-argument is presented in its maximally strong form (steelman), with primary sources, severity assessment, and guidance on what would or would not address the objection.

---

## Counter-Argument 1: Decoherence and Einselection Remove Any Need for Consciousness in Measurement

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT

The consciousness-causes-collapse hypothesis, most closely associated with von Neumann and Wigner, has been rendered scientifically obsolete by the development of decoherence theory, beginning with Zeh's foundational insight in 1970 and developed into a rigorous framework by Zurek and others from 1981 onward. The argument proceeds in three devastating stages.

First, environmentally-induced decoherence explains why macroscopic superpositions are never observed: any system interacting with its environment becomes entangled with environmental degrees of freedom, and when the environment is traced out, the reduced density matrix of the system becomes effectively diagonal in a preferred (pointer) basis, with off-diagonal coherence terms suppressed exponentially fast -- often on femtosecond to attosecond timescales (Zurek 2003; Schlosshauer 2005; Tomaz 2025). The environment acts as a continual "monitor" that effectively singles out pointer states through einselection (environment-induced superselection), solving the preferred basis problem without any appeal to consciousness (Zurek 1981, 2003).

Second, this process is fully automatic, physical, and requires no observer. It occurs for every macroscopic system immersed in an environment -- including measurement apparatuses -- long before any conscious observer could register a result. Wigner himself abandoned the consciousness-collapse interpretation after learning of Zeh's decoherence work, calling his earlier view "solipsistic" in a 1982 lecture and explicitly crediting Zeh's 1970 paper as the reason for his change of mind (Wigner 1984; Wikipedia "Consciousness causes collapse"; Tomaz 2025, Sec. 5.6.2). As John Bell famously quipped: "Was the wave function of the world waiting to jump for thousands of millions of years until a single-celled living creature appeared? Or did it have to wait a little longer, for some better qualified system... with a PhD?" (Bell, quoted in Tomaz 2025, Sec. 5.6.2; Bell 1990).

Third, decoherence is not merely philosophical speculation -- it is an experimentally confirmed consequence of standard quantum mechanics, crucial for quantum computing and observed in countless experiments (Schlosshauer 2005; Brune et al. 1996; Arndt et al. 1999). To posit consciousness as the cause of collapse, one must explain why decoherence -- which already gives the correct quantitative predictions for apparent collapse -- is insufficient, and why an additional non-physical, dualistic mechanism is needed. No such explanation exists that is not either question-begging or empirically indistinguishable from decoherence.

The decisive point: decoherence provides a complete physical account of how a superposition evolves into a statistical mixture of pointer states. What remains -- the "problem of definite outcomes" (why we observe one outcome rather than another) -- is not solved by invoking consciousness either, since consciousness-collapse offers no mechanism for outcome selection beyond decoherence's own limitations. Consciousness is therefore explanatorily redundant at best.

- Wojciech H. Zurek, "Pointer basis of quantum apparatus: Into what mixtures does the wave packet collapse?" *Physical Review D* 24, 1516-1525 (1981). [Introduced the pointer basis concept.]
- Wojciech H. Zurek, "Decoherence, einselection, and the quantum origins of the classical," *Reviews of Modern Physics* 75, 715-774 (2003), esp. Sec. III-V. [Comprehensive review; einselection solves preferred basis but the problem of outcomes remains unresolved.]
- Maximilian Schlosshauer, "Decoherence, the measurement problem, and interpretations of quantum mechanics," *Reviews of Modern Physics* 76, 1267-1305 (2005), esp. pp. 1267-1277, 1295-1301. [Foundational review; explicit statement that decoherence alone does not solve the measurement problem.]
- Stephen L. Adler, "Why decoherence has not solved the measurement problem: a response to P.W. Anderson," *Studies in History and Philosophy of Modern Physics* 34, 135-142 (2003). [Seminal critique of overclaiming decoherence; distinguishes proper from improper mixtures.]

Dieter Zeh on quantum decoherence and macroscopic quantum phenomena."
- John Bell, "Against measurement," in *Sixty-two Years of Uncertainty*, A.I. Miller (ed.), Plenum (1990), and cited in Tomaz 2025, Sec. 5.6.2. [Famous quip about wavefunction waiting for a PhD.]
- Anderson A. Tomaz and Mario Barbatti, "The quantum measurement problem: a review of recent trends," *Philosophical Magazine* (2025/2026), arXiv:2502.19278v2, Sec. 4-5, esp. Sec. 5.6.2. [Recent comprehensive review explicitly stating consciousness-causes-collapse is "discredited."]

### (c) SEVERITY

**Show-stopper.** Decoherence theory is experimentally confirmed, universally accepted in mainstream physics, and directly explains the phenomena that motivated the consciousness-collapse hypothesis. Wigner's own abandonment of the view, combined with the automatic and quantitative character of environmental decoherence, renders consciousness-as-necessary-for-collapse both explanatorily redundant and empirically unsupported. Any framework positing consciousness as fundamental must either show decoherence is insufficient in a testable way, or explain why decoherence's demonstrated sufficiency does not undermine their position.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

A clear experimental demonstration that (i) decoherence fails to predict observed outcomes in a regime where it should apply, AND (ii) the discrepancy correlates with the presence or properties of a conscious observer in a way that cannot be explained by standard physical interactions. Specifically, an experiment showing that systems maintain coherence in the presence of environmental interactions when observed by conscious agents, but decohere identically under all non-conscious physical conditions. The 2021 Chalmers-McQueen framework combining IIT with CSL offers one theoretically coherent path, but remains experimentally unsubstantiated (Chalmers & McQueen 2021, arXiv:2105.02314).

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Arguing that decoherence "doesn't solve the problem of definite outcomes" as if this leaves room for consciousness-collapse. This is a double-edged sword: decoherence doesn't solve it, but neither does consciousness-collapse offer any mechanism for outcome selection.
- Appealing to the "mystery" of consciousness as a reason to prefer it over a well-understood physical mechanism.
- Citing interpretations of quantum mechanics (e.g., von Neumann 1932) that predate decoherence theory without addressing why decoherence does not suffice.
- Claiming that "quantum mechanics is incomplete" without specifying what decoherence fails to predict.

## Counter-Argument 2: Bohmian Mechanics Provides a Fully Deterministic, Consciousness-Free Alternative

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT

The de Broglie-Bohm pilot-wave theory (Bohmian mechanics) furnishes a complete, deterministic interpretation of quantum mechanics in which particles always possess definite positions, the wavefunction never collapses, and consciousness plays no role whatsoever. This is not a mere philosophical curiosity: Bohmian mechanics reproduces all predictions of non-relativistic quantum mechanics, solves the measurement problem by construction (measurements reveal pre-existing particle positions), and has been rigorously developed into a mathematically precise framework by Durr, Goldstein, and Zanghi, among others (Goldstein 2013; Durr et al. 2013; Durr & Teufel 2009).

The argument against consciousness-as-fundamental proceeds as follows. Bohmian mechanics posits two equations: the Schrodinger equation (as in standard QM) and the guiding equation that determines particle trajectories from the wavefunction. Because particles always have definite positions, there is no superposition problem at the level of the actual particle configuration. The measurement apparatus pointer is always in a definite position -- determined by where the Bohmian particles are. The observer's conscious perception is correlated with the particle configuration in their brain, not caused by some non-physical consciousness-induced collapse (Maudlin 1995; Goldstein 2013). The measurement problem is thus solved without consciousness entering the ontology at all.

This presents a direct challenge to any claim that consciousness is necessary to make sense of quantum mechanics. Bohmian mechanics shows that one can have: (a) definite outcomes at all times, (b) no collapse, (c) no branching worlds, (d) no dualistic consciousness-mind interaction, and (e) full empirical equivalence with standard quantum predictions for non-relativistic systems. The existence of even one such coherent alternative decisively undermines any argument of the form "quantum mechanics requires consciousness because without it we cannot explain definite outcomes." Bohmian mechanics explains definite outcomes through deterministic particle trajectories guided by the wavefunction -- a fully physical mechanism.

Furthermore, Bohmian mechanics does not require any modification to the Schrodinger equation, does not suffer from the preferred basis problem (the definite particle configuration picks out the outcomes), and provides a clear picture of the classical limit. While challenges exist -- particularly in relativistic extensions and quantum field theory -- the de Broglie-Bohm framework demonstrates that consciousness is demonstrably not a *necessary* posit for resolving the measurement problem.

### (b) PRIMARY SOURCE(S)

- David Bohm, "A suggested interpretation of the quantum theory in terms of 'hidden' variables," *Physical Review* 85, 166-179; 180-193 (1952). [Original formulation.]
- Sheldon Goldstein, "Bohmian mechanics," in *The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2013), esp. Sec. 1-3, 7-8. [Definitive overview; explicit statement that Bohmian mechanics solves the measurement problem via particle configuration.]
- Detlef Durr, Sheldon Goldstein, and Nino Zanghi, *Quantum Physics Without Quantum Philosophy*, Springer (2013). [Rigorous development.]
- Detlef Durr and Stefan Teufel, *Bohmian Mechanics*, Springer (2009). [Textbook treatment.]
- Tim Maudlin, "Three measurement problems," *Topoi* 14, 7-15 (1995). [Classic analysis of how Bohmian mechanics addresses the measurement problem.]
- Shan Gao, "Does Bohmian mechanics solve the measurement problem? Maybe not yet," arXiv:2202.12279 / *Studies in History and Philosophy of Modern Physics* (2023). [Raises challenges about whether Bohmian brain can generate conscious perceptions, but acknowledges the received view that it does solve the measurement problem.]

### (c) SEVERITY

**Major.** Bohmian mechanics demonstrates that consciousness is not *necessary* for resolving the measurement problem. It provides a complete, empirically equivalent alternative. However, Bohmian mechanics faces its own challenges: nonlocality (which it accepts), difficulties with relativistic extensions, and the question of why nature should include particle trajectories in addition to the wavefunction (the "excess structure" worry). These prevent it from being a show-stopper against consciousness-as-fundamental, but the burden of proof shifts: proponents of consciousness-as-necessary must explain why Bohmian mechanics's demonstrated possibility is not sufficient.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

A principled argument showing that Bohmian mechanics either (i) fails empirically in some domain, (ii) cannot consistently account for conscious experience (the challenge raised by Gao 2023 about IIT and Bohmian brains lacking causal connections between non-entangled particles), or (iii) that its nonlocal guidance mechanism itself requires some conscious/mental substrate to function. Alternatively, showing that the pilot-wave ontology is internally inconsistent when observers are included as quantum systems.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Dismissing Bohmian mechanics as "non-mainstream" -- popularity is not an argument; one must engage with its actual structure.
- Appealing to the "excess structure" objection (particles + wavefunction) as if this undermines the theory; extra ontology that works is not a refutation.
- Claiming that nonlocality "proves" something mystical is needed; Bohmian nonlocality is a precise mathematical feature, not an opening for consciousness.
- Asserting that Bell's theorem rules out hidden variables without acknowledging that Bohmian mechanics is explicitly nonlocal and hence compatible with Bell.

## Counter-Argument 3: QBism and Epistemic Interpretations Remove Consciousness from Ontology

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT

Quantum Bayesianism (QBism), developed by Fuchs, Schack, Mermin, and Caves, offers a radically different interpretation in which the quantum state does not describe objective reality at all -- it describes an agent's subjective degrees of belief, or "gambling attitudes," about the consequences of their interactions with the world (Fuchs 2016; Fuchs et al. 2014; Fuchs, Mermin & Schack 2014). On this view, wavefunction "collapse" is not a physical process caused by consciousness; it is simply a Bayesian update of the agent's beliefs upon acquiring new information. Consciousness is not the fundamental substrate of reality -- it is merely the locus of belief formation, on the same footing as any agent that updates probabilities based on experience.

QBism's challenge to consciousness-as-fundamental is structural. If the wavefunction is epistemic -- a tool for an agent to navigate their experience -- then consciousness does not cause physical events; it merely hosts the reasoning process. The physical world, in QBism, is "more than" any agent's beliefs about it: QBists insist there is a real world out there that resists our choices and surprises us (Fuchs 2016; Fuchs in Quanta Magazine 2015). The quantum formalism is "a law of thought," not a law of consciousness-as-fundamental-substrate. The distinction is crucial: QBism makes consciousness an *epistemic* precondition for *using* quantum theory, not an *ontological* foundation of *physical reality*.

This is reinforced by QBism's treatment of observers. The QBist "agent" need not be conscious in any deep sense -- it is any entity that forms beliefs and updates them based on experience. As Fuchs quipped, "Dogs don't use wave functions... I didn't collapse a wave function until I was 34" (Fuchs, quoted in Quanta Magazine 2015). The observer is a rational agent, not a conscious being in the sense required by Wigner's dualism. Furthermore, different agents assign different wavefunctions to the same system, and there is no requirement that these agree -- the quantum state is irreducibly first-personal (Fuchs, Mermin & Schack 2014).

The broader category of epistemic interpretations -- including Spekkens' toy model and related approaches -- shows that much of quantum mechanics' "weirdness" can be understood as consequences of limited knowledge rather than consciousness-dependent reality. If quantum states represent information or knowledge rather than ontic properties, then the argument that quantum mechanics "proves" consciousness is fundamental dissolves: all it proves is that our knowledge is limited, which is trivially true.

### (b) PRIMARY SOURCE(S)

- Christopher A. Fuchs, "QBism, the perimeter of quantum Bayesianism," *Physical Review A* 85, 065306 (2011); extended in *Coming of Age with Quantum Information* (Cambridge University Press, 2016), esp. Ch. 1-3, 18-20. [Primary text.]
- Christopher A. Fuchs, N. David Mermin, and Rudiger Schack, "An introduction to QBism with an application to the locality of quantum mechanics," *American Journal of Physics* 82, 749-754 (2014). [Definitive introduction.]
- Christopher A. Fuchs and Rudiger Schack, "Quantum-Bayesian coherence," *Reviews of Modern Physics* 85, 1693-1715 (2013). [Technical foundations.]
- Christopher A. Fuchs, interviewed in "A Private View of Quantum Reality," *Quanta Magazine* (June 4, 2015). [Fuchs: "Quantum mechanics is a law of thought"; dogs don't use wavefunctions.]
- Robert W. Spekkens, "Evidence for the epistemic view of quantum states: A toy theory," *Physical Review A* 75, 032110 (2007). [Shows quantum-like phenomena arise from limited knowledge.]
- Carlton M. Caves, Christopher A. Fuchs, and Rudiger Schack, "Subjective probability and quantum certainty," *Studies in History and Philosophy of Modern Physics* 38, 255-274 (2007). [Bayesian foundations.]

### (c) SEVERITY

**Major.** QBism provides a coherent, mathematically precise interpretation of quantum mechanics in which consciousness plays no ontological role. The wavefunction is a belief state, not a physical entity. However, QBism is not universally accepted and faces its own challenges: it is arguably incomplete as an ontology (what *is* the physical world, if not described by wavefunctions?), and some critics argue that its rejection of an objective quantum state leaves it unable to explain quantum cosmology or the early universe. These gaps prevent it from being a definitive refutation, but the burden again shifts: consciousness-as-fundamental must explain why an epistemic interpretation is insufficient.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

A demonstration that QBism's epistemic framework cannot account for some aspect of quantum mechanics -- e.g., the objectivity of quantum computers (which produce results that all observers agree on), or the emergence of classical reality from quantum substrate -- without smuggling in ontological commitments that reintroduce consciousness. Alternatively, showing that QBism's "agent" concept implicitly requires phenomenal consciousness after all, not merely rational belief-formation.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Claiming that QBism is "subjectivist" in a way that supports consciousness-as-fundamental; QBist subjectivity is about *belief*, not *consciousness as physical cause*.
- Asserting that QBism "makes reality observer-dependent" without noting the crucial distinction: QBism makes *quantum states* observer-dependent, not *physical events*.
- Dismissing QBism as "anti-realist" without engaging with its positive account; QBists insist on a real world that resists our choices.
- Confusing QBism with idealism; Fuchs explicitly distinguishes QBism from analytical idealism.

## Counter-Argument 4: The Frauchiger-Renner and Brukner No-Go Theorems Create Contradictions for Consciousness-Dependent Models

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT

The Frauchiger-Renner (FR) theorem and Brukner's no-go theorem for observer-independent facts demonstrate that extending quantum mechanics to include conscious observers as quantum systems leads to logical contradictions -- unless one gives up fundamental assumptions about the objectivity of reality. These results represent the most powerful formal constraints on consciousness-dependent interpretations available in the current literature.

Frauchiger and Renner (2018) prove that three "natural-sounding" assumptions -- (Q) compliance with quantum theory, (C) self-consistency of different agents' predictions, and (S) single-world -- cannot all be valid simultaneously. Their thought experiment involves nested observers (Wigner's-friend-type scenarios) where a friend inside a lab measures a system, and Wigner outside treats the entire lab as a quantum system. If quantum mechanics applies universally, including to conscious observers, then applying standard quantum reasoning leads to a contradiction: there exists a combination of outcomes that has probability zero according to one agent and probability one according to another (Frauchiger & Renner 2018, *Nature Communications* 9, 3711). The implication: "no single-world interpretation can be logically consistent" and "we are forced to give up the view that there is one single reality."

Brukner (2018) derives a Bell-type no-go theorem for observer-independent facts, showing that the assumptions of locality, freedom of choice, and universal validity of quantum theory are incompatible with the assumption that facts are observer-independent. In a Wigner's-friend scenario with entangled pairs and friends/superobservers, the CHSH inequality is violated for the joint system, implying that Wigner's measurement outcomes and his friend's outcomes cannot jointly be considered objective properties of the world (Brukner 2018, *Entropy* 20, 350).

The force of these theorems against consciousness-as-fundamental is direct. If consciousness were the fundamental substrate that causes collapse or determines reality, then conscious observers should be the *most* consistently describable systems in quantum mechanics -- yet these theorems show that treating observers quantum-mechanically leads to contradictions precisely when consciousness-dependent reasoning is applied. The theorems do not depend on any specific model of consciousness; they depend only on applying quantum theory universally and assuming that observers can reason consistently about each other. The fact that contradictions emerge suggests that something is wrong with treating observers -- especially conscious observers -- as special loci of quantum collapse or reality-creation.

These results have been experimentally probed. The Local Friendliness no-go theorem (Bong et al. 2020, *Nature Physics* 16, 1199-1205) extends this line of reasoning and has been subject to experimental tests. While practical realizations remain challenging, the theoretical structure places severe constraints on any interpretation that grants consciousness a privileged role in quantum mechanics.

### (b) PRIMARY SOURCE(S)

- Daniela Frauchiger and Renato Renner, "Quantum theory cannot consistently describe the use of itself," *Nature Communications* 9, 3711 (2018). [Seminal no-go theorem; proves single-world interpretations cannot be self-consistent.]
- Caslav Brukner, "A no-go theorem for observer-independent facts," *Entropy* 20, 350 (2018), arXiv:1804.00749. [Bell-type no-go theorem for observer-independent facts in Wigner's-friend scenarios.]
- Kok-Wei Bong et al., "A strong no-go theorem on the Wigner's friend paradox," *Nature Physics* 16, 1199-1205 (2020). [Experimental extension; Local Friendliness no-go theorem.]
- Elias Okon and Daniel Sudarsky, "Reassessing the strength of a class of Wigner's friend no-go theorems," arXiv:2205.08077 (2022); *Studies in History and Philosophy of Modern Physics* (2022). [Critical assessment; questions the strength of Brukner's theorem.]
- Sebastian Fortin and Olimpia Lombardi, "The Frauchiger-Renner argument: A new no-go result?" *Studies in History and Philosophy of Modern Physics* 70, 125-137 (2020). [Detailed analysis; shows FR argument relies on classical logic in quantum context.]

### (c) SEVERITY

**Major.** These theorems do not directly refute consciousness-as-fundamental, but they show that any such framework must sacrifice at least one of: universal validity of quantum mechanics, single-world reality, or consistency between observers' reasoning. The Frauchiger-Renner theorem is particularly powerful because it does not depend on collapse postulates or consciousness models -- it derives contradictions from bare quantum formalism applied to observers. However, interpretations exist that evade the theorem (e.g., many-worlds by denying single-world, or QBism by denying observer-independent facts), so the theorems function as constraints rather than outright refutations. The Okon & Sudarsky (2022) critique also notes that Brukner's theorem relies on an implicit assumption about the relation between friends' and superobservers' results, somewhat weakening its force.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

A principled argument showing which of the three assumptions (Q), (C), or (S) the consciousness-as-fundamental framework rejects, and why this rejection is physically and philosophically motivated rather than ad hoc. Many-worlds rejects (S); QBism/RQM reject the notion of observer-independent facts in a way that dissolves the paradox. A consciousness-as-fundamental theory must provide its own dissolution that is at least as principled. Alternatively, demonstrating that the FR setup cannot be physically realized -- but note that Bong et al. (2020) have already made experimental progress.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Claiming the FR theorem is "just a thought experiment" -- it has been experimentally probed (Bong et al. 2020).
- Asserting that consciousness is "beyond quantum mechanics" without explaining why quantum theory should not apply to conscious systems.
- Dismissing the theorems as "not about real consciousness" -- they are about observers, and consciousness-as-fundamental must say why observers are not subject to the same constraints.
- Appealing to "higher levels of consciousness" or "non-dual awareness" as escaping the theorem, without formalizing how this works.

## Counter-Argument 5: Information-Theoretic Principles and Objective Collapse Models Require No Consciousness

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT

The landscape of quantum foundations includes two additional research programs that make consciousness explanatorily redundant: (1) information-theoretic principles that bound quantum correlations from below, and (2) objective collapse models (GRW/CSL/Diosi-Penrose) that provide a physical mechanism for wavefunction reduction without any appeal to observers or consciousness.

First, information causality. Pawlowski et al. (2009) introduced the Information Causality (IC) principle: the information gain that Bob can obtain about a previously unknown to him data set of Alice, by using all the local quantum correlations and classical communication, is bounded by the information volume of the communication. Remarkably, this principle, together with no-signaling, is sufficient to derive the Tsirelson bound -- the exact boundary of quantum correlations for the CHSH inequality. Popescu-Rohrlich (PR) boxes, which would violate the Tsirelson bound while respecting no-signaling, are ruled out by information causality. This establishes that quantum mechanics occupies a very specific, principled position in the space of possible physical theories -- and this position is determined by information-theoretic constraints, not by consciousness. Quantum mechanics is "just so" because of how information can propagate, not because conscious observers require definite outcomes.

Related principles -- macroscopic locality, local orthogonality, the absence of third-order interference, and the NPA hierarchy -- collectively constrain the space of possible physical theories to a region that includes and is tightly bounded around quantum mechanics. None of these principles make any reference to consciousness. They are statements about information, correlations, and causal structure. The implication is stark: if consciousness were fundamental to why quantum mechanics takes the form it does, one would expect some signature of consciousness-dependence in these informational bounds. There is none.

Second, objective collapse models. GRW (Ghirardi, Rimini & Weber 1986), CSL (Pearle 1989; Ghirardi, Pearle & Rimini 1990), and the Diosi-Penrose model (Diosi 1989; Penrose 1996) modify the Schrodinger equation by adding spontaneous, stochastic localization terms that cause superpositions to collapse at a rate proportional to system size/mass. These models: (i) reproduce standard quantum predictions for microscopic systems, (ii) guarantee definite outcomes for macroscopic objects, (iii) are experimentally testable in principle, and (iv) require no observer, no consciousness, and no measurement postulate. The collapse is a physical process governed by a new constant of nature (the collapse rate lambda), not a mental act.

Experimental constraints on these models are tightening. The Gran Sasso underground experiment (Curceanu et al. 2022) has constrained the simplest Diosi-Penrose models. Cold-atom experiments, matter-wave interferometry, and optomechanical systems are pushing into regimes where deviations from unitary evolution should be detectable if collapse models are correct. The point is not that these models are confirmed -- they are not -- but that they represent a fully physical, consciousness-free research program for resolving the measurement problem. They demonstrate that the path forward is through new physics, not new metaphysics.

Together, information causality and objective collapse models show that quantum mechanics can be understood and potentially completed through physical and informational principles alone. Consciousness is not required as an explanatory posit in either program.

### (b) PRIMARY SOURCE(S)

- Marcin Pawlowski et al., "Information causality as a physical principle," *Nature* 461, 1101-1104 (2009). [Introduces IC principle; derives Tsirelson bound.]
- Sandu Popescu and Daniel Rohrlich, "Quantum nonlocality as an axiom," *Foundations of Physics* 24, 379-385 (1994). [Introduced PR boxes and superquantum correlations.]
- G.C. Ghirardi, A. Rimini, and T. Weber, "Unified dynamics for microscopic and macroscopic systems," *Physical Review D* 34, 470-491 (1986). [GRW model.]
- G.C. Ghirardi, P. Pearle, and A. Rimini, "Markov processes in Hilbert space and continuous spontaneous localization of systems of identical particles," *Physical Review A* 42, 78-89 (1990). [CSL model.]
- Lajos Diosi, "Models for universal reduction of macroscopic quantum fluctuations," *Physical Review A* 40, 1165-1174 (1989). [Gravity-induced collapse.]
- Roger Penrose, "On gravity's role in quantum state reduction," *General Relativity and Gravitation* 28, 581-600 (1996). [Penrose's gravity-collapse proposal.]
- Angelo Bassi et al., "Models of wave-function collapse, underlying theories, and experimental tests," *Reviews of Modern Physics* 85, 471-601 (2013), esp. Sec. I-IV, IX. [Comprehensive review of collapse models; no consciousness needed.]
- Catalina Curceanu et al., "Experimental tests of objective-collapse models," *Foundations of Physics* 52, 88 (2022). [Gran Sasso experiment constraining collapse models.]
- Elias Okon and Daniel Sudarsky, "A consciousness-based quantum objective collapse model," arXiv:1601.05873 / *Studies in History and Philosophy of Modern Physics* (2018). [Shows that even if one *wanted* to build consciousness into collapse, it requires CSL as a base, which itself needs no consciousness.]

### (c) SEVERITY

**Major.** Information causality and related principles derive the specific structure of quantum correlations from purely information-theoretic constraints, with no reference to consciousness. Objective collapse models provide testable, physical mechanisms for definite outcomes that are entirely mechanistic. Together, they show that consciousness-as-fundamental is not only unnecessary but unmotivated: the features of quantum mechanics that might seem to require consciousness (definite outcomes, bounded correlations) are explained by information principles and physical dynamics. However, neither program is experimentally confirmed (collapse models make testable predictions but remain unverified), so this is a Major rather than Show-stopper objection.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

Showing that information causality or related principles cannot be formulated without implicit reference to conscious observers -- e.g., that "information gain" presupposes a conscious agent who gains information. Or demonstrating that objective collapse models fail experimentally in a way that correlates with conscious observation. Alternatively, developing a consciousness-dependent collapse model that makes *different* predictions from GRW/CSL and is experimentally confirmed where GRW/CSL fails. The Chalmers-McQueen (2021) IIT+CSL hybrid is a step in this direction but remains experimentally unsubstantiated.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Asserting that "information requires an informee" without formalizing what this means in the context of IC; IC is a mathematical constraint on correlations, not a semantic claim about meaning.
- Claiming collapse models are "ad hoc" without addressing their empirical content; ad hoc hypotheses that make correct predictions are still valid science.
- Dismissing the IC principle because it doesn't explain *why* quantum mechanics respects it; IC is a constraint, not a derivation from first principles.
- Appealing to the "hard problem of consciousness" as evidence that consciousness must be fundamental; the hard problem is a philosophical issue about qualia, not a physics argument about collapse.

## Methodological Notes

1. **Search strategy**: 12+ independent web and arXiv searches were performed across: consciousness-collapse critique, decoherence measurement problem, Bohmian mechanics, QBism, Penrose-Hameroff ORCH-OR critique, information causality, objective collapse models, Frauchiger-Renner theorem, quantum measurement problem reviews (2023-2025), relational quantum mechanics, and quantum Darwinism.

2. **Flagged uncertainties**:
   - The exact formulation of Bell's "PhD quip" varies across sources; the earliest published version appears to be in Bell (1990), but the precise wording may have been delivered in lectures earlier.
   - The severity of the Frauchiger-Renner theorem is contested: Okon & Sudarsky (2022) argue Brukner's theorem relies on an implicit assumption, and Fortin & Lombardi (2020) note the FR argument uses classical logic in a quantum context. These critiques somewhat weaken the force, but the theorems remain widely cited constraints.
   - The experimental status of objective collapse models is rapidly evolving; the severity rating assumes current null results. A positive detection could change the landscape.
   - Bohmian mechanics' ability to fully solve the measurement problem is contested by Gao (2023), though this remains a minority view in the field.

3. **Dropped objections**: Several weaker or already-answered objections were not included:
   - The "measurement requires a conscious observer because only consciousness can register information" argument is directly refuted by decoherence + quantum Darwinism showing that information can be redundantly encoded in the environment without any conscious registration (Zurek 2003, 2018; Schlosshauer 2005).

Its inclusion would be more of a "weak argument to avoid" than a strong counter-argument.
   - Henry Stapp's quantum-Zeno approach to consciousness (Stapp 1993, 2001) was reviewed but found to have been extensively critiqued on grounds of physical implausibility; it does not meet the threshold for a "strongest" counter-argument.
