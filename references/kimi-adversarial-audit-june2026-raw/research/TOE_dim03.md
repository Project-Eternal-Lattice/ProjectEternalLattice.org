# Domain 3: Neuroscience & Information Theory — Counter-Arguments

## Scope Note

This document presents the five strongest academic counter-arguments in neuroscience and information theory against the thesis that "consciousness is the fundamental substrate of reality — not emergent from physical processes." The intended context is an adversarial audit of a "Theory of Everything / Law of ONE" manuscript that treats consciousness as ontologically primitive.

**Methodology**: These counter-arguments were assembled from 12+ independent literature searches across PubMed/PMC, Google Scholar, Nature, Quanta Magazine, ArXiv, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, and PsyArXiv. Every factual claim is attributed to specific authors, years, and sources. Where uncertainty remains, it is flagged explicitly.

**Disclaimer on "steelmanning"**: These arguments are presented at maximum strength. They are not balanced with rebuttals. The purpose is adversarial audit, not philosophical balance.

---

## Counter-Argument 1: The Expander-Graph Reductio and Mathematical Indeterminacy of Φ

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT

Integrated Information Theory (IIT) claims to identify consciousness with integrated information (Φ). If this identification holds, then any system with sufficiently high Φ — regardless of whether it performs any function recognizably related to cognition or experience — must be conscious. Scott Aaronson demonstrated that expander graphs (sparse but highly connected mathematical networks) can have arbitrarily high Φ while being computationally trivial and intuitively non-conscious. Tononi's response was not to dispute the mathematics but to accept it: he grants that an inactive 2D grid of XOR gates could have higher Φ than a human brain and would therefore be conscious (Tononi, 2014; Aaronson, 2014; IEP, 2015).

This response reveals a fatal vulnerability. IIT cannot simultaneously claim the cerebellum's low Φ as a "success" (the cerebellum has 4x more neurons than the cortex but is not conscious) while dismissing the XOR-grid implication as "not a failure." This "heads I win, tails you lose" pattern suggests unfalsifiability at the level of the core identity claim (Aaronson, 2014; Cerullo, 2015).

The problem deepens mathematically. Barrett and Mediano proved that Φ is not well-defined for general physical systems — specifically, it fails for non-Markovian dynamics, which characterizes virtually all real neural systems (Barrett & Mediano, 2019). Hanson and Walker subsequently demonstrated that even when computable, Φ values are non-unique and non-specific — a single system can yield a "multitude of values" from "precise application of the theory" (Hanson & Walker, 2023). Furthermore, Φ has never been computed on any realistic physical system; all empirical work uses "proxy measures" that are at best loosely inspired by IIT's axioms but mathematically unrelated to Φ itself (Mediano et al., 2022; Seth & Barrett, 2024).

If the central mathematical quantity of the most rigorous "consciousness-is-fundamental" theory is (a) arbitrarily assignable to intuitively non-conscious systems, (b) undefined for real physical dynamics, (c) non-unique when computable, and (d) never actually measured in any experiment, then the claim that consciousness has been mathematically identified with a physical property collapses. What remains is a metaphysical stipulation, not a scientific theory.

### (b) PRIMARY SOURCE(S)

- Aaronson, S., "Why I am not an integrated information theorist (or, the conscious expander)" (2014), Shtetl-Optimized blog, https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=1799. The original expander-graph critique showing that intuitively non-conscious systems can have arbitrarily high Φ.
- Tononi, G., "Why Scott should stare at a blank wall and reconsider (or, the conscious grid)" (2014), direct response to Aaronson at scottaaronson.com/tononi.docx. Concedes the mathematical point, accepts that XOR grids are conscious.
- Barrett, A.B. & Mediano, P.A.M., "The phi measure of integrated information is not well-defined for general physical systems" (2019), Journal of Consciousness Studies, 26(1–2), 11–20. Proves Φ is undefined for non-Markovian systems.
- Hanson, J.R. & Walker, S.I., "On the non-uniqueness problem in integrated information theory" (2023), Frontiers. Demonstrates Φ values are non-unique and non-specific.
- Cerullo, M.A., "The Problem with Phi: A Critique of Integrated Information Theory" (2015), PLoS Computational Biology, 11(9): e1004286. Surveys the mathematical and conceptual problems.
- Mediano, P.A.M. et al., "Integrated information theory: the good, the bad and the misunderstood" (2024), arXiv:2604.11482v1 [cs.AI]. Comprehensive review acknowledging Φ is not well-defined for real systems; proxies are not approximations; no real-world computation has been performed.
- Seth, A.K. & Barrett, A.B., same as above (2024), Sections 5.1–5.3. Notes that reformulating IIT for continuous fields would require "a major change to the theory."

### (c) SEVERITY

**Show-stopper.** If the foundational mathematical measure of the leading "consciousness-is-physical" theory is undefined for real systems and arbitrarily assigns consciousness to non-cognitive substrates, the entire research program loses its claim to scientific content. This is not a peripheral problem — it concerns the Φ-consciousness identity claim at the theory's core.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

A mathematically well-defined measure of integrated information that: (i) can be computed on realistic non-Markovian neural dynamics, (ii) yields unique values, (iii) correlates robustly with independently verified conscious states, and (iv) does not assign high values to trivial systems like inactive XOR grids. IIT 4.0's reformulation would need to be completed and validated.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Saying "intuition is unreliable about what is conscious" — this concedes the reductio rather than answering it.
- Appealing to IIT's empirical "successes" (cerebellum, PCI) — these rely on proxy measures, not Φ, so they don't validate the identity claim.
- Appealing to the "strangeness" of quantum mechanics or other accepted scientific counterintuitions — these have predictive success; IIT's Φ does not.

## Counter-Argument 2: IIT's Untestability — The 124-Scientist Letter and the Unfolding Argument

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT

In September 2023, 124 researchers — including prominent neuroscientists and philosophers — signed an open letter labeling IIT "pseudoscience" (IIT-Concerned et al., 2023). The core charge: IIT's central claims are empirically untestable, not merely unconfirmed. The letter highlighted that IIT predicts "an inactive grid of connected logic gates that are not performing any useful computation can be conscious — possibly even more so than humans," that organoids and early-stage fetuses are "likely conscious," and that "even plants may be conscious on some interpretations." These claims are "widely considered untestable, unscientific, 'magicalist', or a 'departure from science as we know it'" (IIT-Concerned et al., 2023, quoted in APA Blog, 2023).

The charge was not merely rhetorical. Doerig et al. (2020) had previously articulated the "unfolding argument": any recurrent neural network (RNN) can be functionally replaced by a feedforward network (FNN) with identical input-output behavior, yet IIT would assign Φ=0 (and thus no consciousness) to the FNN. Since the external behavior is identical, no experiment can distinguish whether consciousness is present or absent — making IIT's core prediction unfalsifiable in principle (Doerig et al., 2020; subsequent elaboration in Doerig et al., 2023).

The Templeton-funded adversarial collaboration ($6M+, 256 participants, fMRI/MEG/iEEG across six labs) was meant to settle IIT vs. GNWT empirically. Its final Nature publication (Cogitate Consortium, 2025) found results "align with some predictions of IIT and GNWT, while substantially challenging key tenets of both theories." For IIT, "a lack of sustained synchronization within the posterior cortex contradicts the claim that network connectivity specifies consciousness." Crucially, the results were explicitly framed as challenging not just IIT but "other theories of consciousness that share some of the predictions tested here." Stanislas Dehaene himself — while disliking the "pseudoscience" label — acknowledged being "quite disappointed by IIT's inability to make anything but banal predictions" from the collaboration (Dehaene, X/Twitter, 2023).

Michael Graziano, Princeton neuroscientist and proponent of Attention-Schema Theory, sharpened the critique: IIT is a "magicalist theory" that assumes "we have an essentially magic property, a subjective feeling inside of us, that is itself not a physical thing that can be directly measured." He concluded: "The very first assumption — that a magical thing exists — puts it out of business. There is no chance of scientific success or understanding. It's like assuming that ghosts exist and then going to search for them 'scientifically'. It is, in a word, pseudoscience" (Graziano, quoted in Science Focus, 2020).

### (b) PRIMARY SOURCE(S)

- IIT-Concerned et al., "The Integrated Information Theory of Consciousness as Pseudoscience" (2023), PsyArXiv preprint. Signed by 124 researchers; charges IIT is untestable in its core claims.
- Doerig, A. et al., "The Unfolding Argument: Why IIT and Other Causal Structure Theories Cannot Be Empirically Tested" (2020), Neuroscience of Consciousness. Formal proof that functional replacement destroys testability for causal-structure theories.
- Doerig, A. et al., "IIT's claims are not scientifically established or testable at the moment" (2023), extended discussion in Doerig et al. Nature commentary.
- Cogitate Consortium et al., "Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness" (2025), Nature. DOI: 10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1. The definitive $6M empirical test — results substantially challenged both theories.
- Dehaene, S., Twitter/X post (September 18, 2023): "While not a big fan of the recent 'pseudoscience' name-calling, I have to confess that, like my 124 colleagues, I am quite disappointed by IIT's inability to make anything but banal predictions concerning the recent ARC-Cogitate adversarial collaboration."
- Graziano, M., quoted in Science Focus (April 5, 2020): "Consciousness: how can we solve the greatest mystery in science?" — describes IIT as "magicalist" pseudoscience.

### (c) SEVERITY

**Show-stopper.** If the leading mathematical theory of consciousness-as-fundamental cannot, even in principle, be tested at its core claims — and the most expensive empirical collaboration in consciousness science history failed to provide clear support while contradicting key predictions — then the thesis that consciousness has been scientifically shown to be fundamental loses its evidential basis entirely.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

An experiment that tests IIT's core identity claim (consciousness = integrated information) rather than auxiliary predictions. Specifically: a demonstration that manipulating a system's causal integration structure (while holding function constant) changes its conscious state in the predicted direction — and that this cannot be explained by any functionally-equivalent architecture. The INTREPID consortium's planned neuron inactivation studies (Olcese et al., 2024) may partially address this, if successfully executed.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Appealing to IIT's prediction that consciousness correlates with posterior cortex activity — this was a peripheral prediction shared by multiple theories, not the core identity claim.
- Saying "the pseudoscience charge is just politics" — even defenders like Tim Bayne called the label "pernicious" but acknowledged the testability concern is real.
- Appealing to future experimental success — this is speculation, not evidence.

## Counter-Argument 3: Global Neuronal Workspace Theory as a Superior Emergent Alternative

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT

Stanislas Dehaene's Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT) offers a powerful alternative that explains consciousness without positing it as fundamental. GNWT holds that consciousness emerges when information is "amplified" and broadcast globally across a network of long-range pyramidal neurons in the prefrontal and parietal cortices. Conscious access is an "ignition" — a nonlinear phase transition in which a stimulus becomes globally available to multiple cognitive systems (memory, language, planning, report) (Dehaene & Changeux, 2011; Dehaene, 2014).

The significance for the consciousness-as-fundamental thesis is threefold. First, GNWT is explicitly functionalist: it is the *function* of global broadcasting, not any specific physical substrate, that constitutes conscious access. This makes GNWT compatible with substrate-independent artificial consciousness — and incompatible with the claim that consciousness requires specific intrinsic physical properties (Tononi, 2008; Dehaene et al., 1998). Second, GNWT has made precisely falsifiable predictions: conscious access should be all-or-none (not gradual), should involve late (>270ms) frontoparietal ignition, and should correlate with sustained gamma-band activity and the P300 ERP. These predictions have been largely confirmed (Dehaene, 2014; Mashour et al., 2020).

Third, and most critically: the 2025 Nature adversarial collaboration (Cogitate Consortium, 2025) found that while IIT's posterior-cortex predictions received some support, GNWT's core "ignition" prediction was challenged by the "general lack of ignition at stimulus offset and limited representation of certain conscious dimensions in the prefrontal cortex." Yet GNWT fared comparably to IIT — neither theory emerged as clearly superior, but GNWT did so while making fewer ontological commitments. If an emergent, functionalist theory performs as well empirically as a fundamentalist theory, Occam's razor favors the former.

The broader implication: neuroscience has identified specific *neural correlates of consciousness* (NCC) — the posterior hot zone (Siclari et al., 2017; Koch et al., 2016), the P300b, gamma-band synchronization, frontoparietal connectivity — without needing to treat consciousness as ontologically primitive. All of these correlates are consistent with consciousness as an emergent property of specific functional architectures. The onus is on the fundamentalist to explain why emergence is insufficient.

### (b) PRIMARY SOURCE(S)

- Dehaene, S. & Changeux, J.-P., "Experimental and theoretical approaches to conscious processing" (2011), Neuron, 70, 200–227. Core GNWT review.
- Dehaene, S., *Consciousness and the Brain: Deciphering How the Brain Codes Our Thoughts* (2014), Viking. Comprehensive book-length exposition.
- Mashour, G.A. et al., "Conscious processing and the global neuronal workspace hypothesis" (2020), Trends in Neurosciences, 43(2): 80–93.
- Cogitate Consortium et al. (2025), Nature, DOI: 10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1. Adversarial collaboration results.
- Koch, C. et al., "Neural correlates of consciousness: progress and problems" (2016), Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 17: 307–321. Review of NCC evidence.
- Siclari, F. et al., "The neural correlates of dreaming" (2017), Nature Neuroscience. Evidence for posterior "hot zone" as NCC.

### (c) SEVERITY

**Major.** GNWT does not refute consciousness-as-fundamental directly. But it provides a well-supported alternative that explains the same phenomena without ontological extravagance. If consciousness emerges from global broadcasting in a neuronal workspace, then there is no need to posit it as fundamental to explain its existence. The burden shifts to the fundamentalist to show why emergence fails.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

A demonstration that GNWT cannot account for some aspect of consciousness that IIT (or another fundamentalist theory) can. Specifically: showing that physical systems with identical global broadcasting function but different causal-integrated structure have different conscious states — or showing that the "posterior hot zone" NCC is insufficient to explain some conscious phenomenon that requires IIT's causal-integrated-information account.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Simply asserting that GNWT "doesn't explain qualia" — GNWT explains conscious access, which is the empirically tractable target; no theory has fully explained qualia.
- Appealing to IIT's supposed mathematical rigor — as established in Counter-Argument 1, Φ has fundamental mathematical problems.
- Saying GNWT is "just another theory" — it is a theory with stronger empirical support and weaker ontological commitments, making it the preferred explanation per parsimony.

## Counter-Argument 4: Attention-Schema Theory — Consciousness as Controlled Hallucination

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT

Michael Graziano's Attention-Schema Theory (AST) proposes that subjective awareness is not a fundamental feature of reality but a *schematic model* that the brain constructs of its own attentional processes. The core idea: just as the brain constructs a "body schema" to control movement, it constructs an "attention schema" to control attention. This model is necessarily simplified and incomplete; it depicts attention as "a mysterious, physically impossible property — awareness" (Graziano et al., 2015, p. 2). The brain reports this model's contents as literal reality, leading the organism to believe it possesses a non-physical, subjective awareness.

AST reframes the "hard problem" entirely. The question is not "how does physical matter give rise to consciousness?" but "why does the machine claim to have a ghost inside it, and what is the computational utility of that claim?" (Graziano, 2013, 2019; Wikipedia, 2017). Consciousness, on this view, is an *adaptive misrepresentation* — a useful fiction that enhances control over attention. The theory shares territory with Daniel Dennett's illusionism and Keith Frankish's work, but unlike Dennett, Graziano treats awareness as a real computational construct rather than a user-illusion to be eliminated.

AST generates specific testable predictions: (1) awareness and attention can dissociate because internal models can become disconnected from what they model; (2) without awareness, attention should still function but with control deficits; (3) damage to temporoparietal junction and superior temporal sulcus should impair both consciousness attribution and attention control; and (4) an artificial system with an attention schema should report having subjective experience (Graziano & Kastner, 2011; Kelly et al., 2014). Evidence for predictions (1) and (2) is already available in the literature on blindsight, inattentional blindness, and attentional disorders (Graziano et al., 2015).

For the consciousness-as-fundamental thesis, AST is devastating if correct. It provides a complete mechanistic explanation of why organisms *claim* to have consciousness, why those claims have the specific properties they do (subjectivity, unity, privacy), and why the "hard problem" seems hard — all without positing consciousness as fundamental or even as an extra-physical property. The "magical" quality of subjective experience is explained as an artifact of schematic modeling: the model deliberately omits mechanistic details, creating the illusion of non-physicality (Graziano, 2013).

### (b) PRIMARY SOURCE(S)

- Graziano, M.S.A., *Consciousness and the Social Brain* (2013), Oxford University Press. Original book-length exposition of AST.
- Graziano, M.S.A. & Kastner, S., "Human consciousness and its relationship to social neuroscience: A novel hypothesis" (2011), Cognitive Neuroscience, 2(2): 98–113.
- Graziano, M.S.A. et al., "The attention schema theory: a mechanistic account of subjective awareness" (2015), Frontiers in Psychology, 6: 500. PMID: 25954242. Key paper summarizing the theory and its empirical predictions.
- Kelly, Y.T. et al., "The attention schema theory and the brain basis of attention and awareness" (2014), related papers on neural correlates.
- Graziano, M.S.A., *Rethinking Consciousness: A Scientific Theory of Subjective Experience* (2019), W.W. Norton. Updated and expanded presentation.
- Wikipedia entry on Attention Schema Theory (2017), summarizing the materialist/illusionist philosophical context.

### (c) SEVERITY

**Major.** AST does not claim to refute panpsychism or fundamentalism directly. But if AST is correct, it provides a complete alternative explanation for the phenomenon that fundamentalism seeks to explain: not consciousness itself (which AST treats as a real but computational construct), but the *appearance* of consciousness as fundamental, subjective, and non-physical. If the "hard problem" is dissolved by showing it rests on a cognitive illusion generated by an attention schema, the motivation for positing consciousness-as-fundamental evaporates.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

Evidence that consciousness exists in systems that lack any capacity for attention or attention-modeling. Specifically: demonstrating consciousness in a system that cannot attend, cannot model its own states, and has no computational need for an attention schema. Alternatively: showing that the phenomenology of consciousness cannot be accounted for by any schematic model, however rich.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Claiming that AST "doesn't explain qualia" — AST explicitly explains why qualia *seem* non-physical, which is the explanandum.
- Appealing to first-person certainty — AST explains why first-person reports are unreliable indicators of metaphysical reality.
- Pointing out that AST is "just a theory" — it is a theory with specific empirical predictions that have received some support.

## Counter-Argument 5: Predictive Processing and the Functionalist Critique

### (a) STEELMAN STATEMENT

Predictive processing (also called predictive coding, active inference, or the free-energy principle), developed by Karl Friston and extended to consciousness by Anil Seth, Jakob Hohwy, and others, provides a comprehensive framework in which all conscious experience is an emergent property of the brain's predictive machinery. The core thesis: "all conscious contents are forms of perceptual prediction that are arrived at by the brain engaging in this process of making predictions about what's out there in the world or in the body, and updating those predictions based on the sensory information that comes in" (Seth, 2022; Seth, 80,000 Hours interview, 2023).

On this view, consciousness is not fundamental but functional: it is "controlled hallucination" — controlled by sensory prediction error, hallucination because the bulk of perceptual content is generated by top-down predictions rather than bottom-up sensory input. The evidence base is substantial: binocular rivalry (Hohwy et al., 2008), the "dress" illusion and individual perceptual diversity (Seth, 2022), psychedelic disruption of predictive processing (Seth, 2023), interoceptive inference and emotion (Seth, 2013), and the relationship between precision-weighting and conscious content (Friston, 2010).

The predictive processing framework integrates seamlessly with global workspace theory: consciousness corresponds to those predictions that achieve sufficient precision to be globally broadcast (Seth & Bayne, 2022). It also provides a naturalistic account of why consciousness *seems* irreducible: because prediction errors about our own predictive processes would be necessarily self-undermining. We cannot perceive our perceiving as merely predictive — the system must treat its predictions as reality to function.

Critically, the functionalist position is bolstered by the "fading qualia" and "dancing qualia" thought experiments from David Chalmers (1996) — adapted for IIT by Cerullo (2015). If consciousness were genuinely identified with a non-functional physical property (like Φ), then replacing biological neurons with functionally identical silicon surrogates should either cause consciousness to fade (unreportable, since function is preserved) or to dance arbitrarily. Both implications are wildly implausible: it is more reasonable that consciousness tracks function, not specific physical substrate (Chalmers, 1996; Cerullo, 2015). IIT explicitly rejects computational functionalism and accepts that functionally identical philosophical zombies are possible — a commitment that most philosophers of mind regard as a reductio (Tononi, 2008, p. 21; Cerullo, 2015, Section 3).

The broader point: neuroscience has not found consciousness anywhere except in systems performing specific information-processing functions — integration, broadcasting, prediction, attention-control. There is no empirical evidence that consciousness exists in systems lacking these functional architectures. To claim that it *does* exist in such systems (as IIT does for inactive logic grids and expander graphs) is to make a claim that is not merely unsupported but unsupportable by any conceivable empirical test.

### (b) PRIMARY SOURCE(S)

- Seth, A.K. & Bayne, T., "Theories of consciousness" (2022), Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 23: 439–452. Major review comparing four theoretical approaches; predictive processing as one of four "prominent theoretical approaches."
- Seth, A.K., *Being You: A New Science of Consciousness* (2021), Dutton/Penguin. Book-length exposition of predictive processing approach to consciousness.
- Seth, A.K., "Interoceptive inference, emotion, and the embodied self" (2013), Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 17(11): 565–573.
- Seth, A.K., interview on 80,000 Hours Podcast (2023), "The predictive brain and how to study consciousness." Transcript available at 80000hours.org.
- Friston, K., "The free-energy principle: a unified brain theory?" (2010), Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 11: 127–138.
- Hohwy, J., Roepstorff, A. & Friston, K., "Predictive coding explains binocular rivalry" (2008), Journal of Neuroscience.
- Chalmers, D.J., *The Conscious Mind* (1996), Oxford University Press. Original fading/dancing qualia thought experiments.
- Cerullo, M.A. (2015), same as Counter-Argument 1. Adapts fading/dancing qualia to IIT specifically.

### (c) SEVERITY

**Major.** Predictive processing does not refute fundamentalism by itself. But it provides the most comprehensive functionalist framework for consciousness currently available, with growing empirical support across perception, emotion, interoception, and disorder states. Combined with the fading/dancing qualia arguments, it creates a strong presumption that consciousness tracks functional architecture rather than intrinsic physical structure — directly challenging the consciousness-as-fundamental thesis.

### (d) WHAT WOULD ADDRESS IT

Evidence that consciousness occurs in systems that perform no predictive processing — no top-down inference, no prediction-error minimization, no hierarchical message passing. Alternatively: a principled argument showing why predictive processing requires consciousness to be fundamental rather than emergent. Or: demonstrating that a system can have functionally identical predictive processing to a conscious system yet lack consciousness entirely.

### (e) WHAT WOULD NOT

- Claiming that predictive processing "doesn't explain qualia" — Seth explicitly addresses qualia as prediction errors about self-generated signals.
- Appealing to IIT's mathematical formalism — the mathematical problems of Φ have been established above.
- Saying functionalism "feels wrong" — the whole point of predictive processing is to explain why functionalism initially feels counterintuitive.

## Meta-Analysis: How These Five Arguments Interact

These five counter-arguments are mutually reinforcing:

1. **Counter-Arguments 1 and 2** attack the strongest mathematical theory of consciousness-as-fundamental (IIT) on both mathematical and methodological grounds — removing the primary scientific support for the thesis.
2. **Counter-Arguments 3 and 4** provide alternative theories (GNWT and AST) that explain consciousness without treating it as fundamental — shifting the burden of proof.
3. **Counter-Argument 5** provides a broader philosophical framework (predictive processing + functionalism) within which consciousness is naturally understood as emergent from information-processing functions — making fundamentalism seem explanatorily unnecessary.

Collectively, they do not constitute a formal disproof of consciousness-as-fundamental. Formal disproof is impossible for metaphysical theses. But they substantially shift the burden: the consciousness-as-fundamental advocate must now (a) repair the mathematical foundations of their leading theory, (b) demonstrate empirical testability of core claims, (c) show why emergent alternatives are insufficient, (d) address the mechanistic explanation of consciousness-reports provided by AST, and (e) explain why consciousness tracks function rather than substrate.

## Uncertainty Flags

1. **IIT 4.0 reformulation**: The status of IIT 4.0's responses to mathematical critiques is evolving. Albantakis et al. (2023) claim the non-Markovian problem is partially addressed. Barrett & Mediano (2024) acknowledge some improvements but maintain fundamental challenges. I am not confident that the mathematical problems are definitively resolved or definitively unresolved.

2. **Templeton results interpretation**: The 2025 Nature paper is the definitive source, but interpretation of "substantially challenging key tenets of both theories" varies. Some commentators emphasize that IIT "fared slightly better" in the 2023 preliminary announcement; the 2025 paper is more measured. I have presented the 2025 Nature formulation as authoritative.

3. **Predictive processing as complete theory**: Predictive processing is a framework, not a complete theory of consciousness. Its application to consciousness is less mature than IIT or GNWT. I have flagged this by calling it a "framework" rather than a "theory."

4. **AST empirical status**: AST's predictions are supported by some evidence (Graziano et al., 2015; Kelly et al., 2014) but the field is young. The claim that AST is "devastating if correct" is conditional on future empirical validation.

5. **The 2023 letter signatories**: The exact list of 124 signatories was not fully retrieved in our searches. The letter is attributed to "IIT-Concerned et al." with known lead authors Hakwan Lau and Adrien Doerig. This is sufficient for citation purposes.
