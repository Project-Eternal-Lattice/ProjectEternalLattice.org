# The Unreachable Floor

## The Plain-Language Companion to *The Physics Core, Paper I* (v1.3)

## We Had to Redraw the Map

Here’s what happened since the last edition, told straight, because this project runs on telling it straight.

A fourth outside inspector — a different AI again, with no stake in being kind — checked our paper and found that one of our labels contradicted itself: we’d written down a compass convention and a measurement that couldn’t both be true. Three earlier inspectors had missed it, because each had trusted the label instead of re-measuring. The fourth re-measured.

Fixing that label forced us back to the original survey — the source book this whole map was drawn from. And there we found something bigger than a label. The original survey draws the landscape in a particular *projection* — a particular way of laying the territory onto paper — and somewhere along the way, in our own rewriting, we had quietly switched to a different projection without flagging it. Same warp rule, same landmarks, different map. Every inspection since had checked our map faithfully — and our map was internally perfect — but it wasn’t the *book’s* map.

So we redrew everything in the original’s projection, re-proved every line by machine before writing a word, and put the whole correction in print with the author’s name on the mistake. That’s this edition.

## What Survived the Redraw — Everything That Matters Most

Here is the remarkable part: **the heart of the map didn’t move.**

The door is still a door: there’s still a strict minimum drive below which no traveler can exist at all — on or off, no dimmer. Every traveler still bounces: approach the glowing floor with all the drive you carry, and the landscape turns you around at an exact closest-approach point. The see-saw law still holds: drive and depth still trade against each other in a perfect fixed balance. And the mirror is still a mirror: the rule connecting drive to depth is still self-inverse — feed it its own answer and it returns your question — with its single self-balancing point still sitting at that famous threshold number from quantum physics.

All of that was never about the projection. It lives in the warp rule itself, which both maps share. The crown jewels came through untouched — and we re-verified each one in the new projection anyway, because trust is recomputation.

## What Changed — and One Change Is Astonishing

The steepness numbers changed: the floor in the true projection is still infinitely curved, but it ramps up more gently as you approach. The push-back at the bounce got *more* beautiful: in the corrected map, the strength of the turnaround follows a perfect inverse-cube law — bounce twice as deep, get pushed back eight times as hard — a law so clean it looks designed, and nobody designed it.

But the big change is the fate of **light**.

In our old projection, light could touch the floor. In the original’s projection — the true one — **light never arrives.** A ray can fly toward the floor for an eternity of its own time and the floor recedes before it forever. We can even tell you the honest reason: near the floor, the landscape stretches every step of light’s journey more and more, so the remaining distance, as light itself experiences it, never runs out. It’s a known kind of magic — mathematicians proved decades ago that stretching a map this way can put an edge beyond light’s reach — and our paper says so plainly and cites them, because the marvel isn’t the trick, it’s *this particular landscape* doing it.

Meanwhile the humble surveyor still wins: a measuring chain, laid out link by link straight toward the floor, **arrives in a finite number of links**. So the corrected map’s deepest truth is stranger than the old one’s:

**The floor of this world is near enough to measure, too far for light, and forbidden to every traveler.** Coasting lives bounce away from it. Light chases it forever. Only the ruler touches it.

## The Equation That Journeyed Three Times

One formula has now made three journeys through this project, and all three are in the paper.

First we retired it: a full search proved it appears nowhere in the source book, so everything built on it was honestly dismantled. Then we welcomed it home: while writing the first edition of this very paper, we derived it fresh — and celebrated, because the lost equation seemed to have been a true consequence of the book all along. And now we’ve retired it a second time: the homecoming was real mathematics about the *wrong projection*. In the book’s true projection, the curvature follows a different formula — the one now proven in this edition.

Retired correctly. Recovered incorrectly. Re-retired correctly. All three chapters in print, with names attached. Because a map you can trust is one that shows you not just its territory but its erasures.

## Why This Makes the Paper Stronger, Not Weaker

You might think a correction this deep would weaken a paper headed for one of the world’s great mathematicians. We believe — and our own hostile review process concluded — the opposite. Every claim in this edition was re-proven by machine before it was written down. Every predecessor we could find is named before any reviewer can name them at us. Every error we made is disclosed with its author. And the genuinely new things — the see-saw mirror with its quantum balance point, the inverse-cube bounce, the floor with three different fates for three kinds of paths — survived every demolition we could throw at them.

One more inspection came back as this edition went to press: two inspectors passed the corrected map outright, and the fourth — the same one who re-measured the compass — found a final flaw: one of our sentences contradicted another, three pages apart. We had described the floor as resisting a certain kind of mathematical smoothing, while our own explanation of light’s endless chase *depends* on exactly that smoothing being possible. The inspector was right. The fix made the truth sharper, not duller: the floor is genuinely broken to physics — no traveler’s world extends past it — yet perfectly smooth to the deeper skeleton beneath the map, and *that very smoothness* is what lets it flee from light forever. The same inspector also asked us to use slightly more careful words in two places, and another suggested showing one extra line of arithmetic in a proof; we checked that line was already right, then showed it anyway. Being checkable is the product.

That’s not a paper that got caught. That’s a paper that catches itself. It goes back for one more independent inspection before it travels anywhere — that’s the standard, and the standard is the point.
