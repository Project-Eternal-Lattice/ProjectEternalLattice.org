## How He Thinks

### 1. Teaches Through Analogy
- Fish-in-the-pond (anthropic principle)
- Toddler smashing a toy (particle physics)
- Dark room with a door (theoretical exploration)
- Sheet of paper universe (branes/multiverse)
- Tycho Brahe & Kepler story (experimental → theoretical)

**Implication:** The band/genre-locking analogy will land naturally. He speaks the same language.

### 2. Values Honest Limitations
> "String theory is speculative, we should be very careful about trusting it until there's experimental checks."

He openly admits string theory can't make predictions yet. A man who acknowledges his own framework's limits will **respect** a paper that does the same honestly.

**Implication:** The tiered epistemic framework (Tier 1: empirical, Tier 2: derived, Tier 3: conjecture) will read as intellectual maturity, not weakness.

### 3. Thinks in Patterns and Symmetry
> "Physics in a nutshell is cataloging the symmetries of nature. Everything we do in physics is just that."

His entire philosophy of physics = finding patterns.

**Implication:** Frame the scaling-law classification as discovering a **pattern** — a symmetry between entanglement structure and gravitational dynamics:
- Area-law → dilaton gravity
- Log-law → critical/conformal
- Volume-law → no coherent geometry

### 4. Comfortable with "We Don't Know Yet"
He holds string theory loosely. Acknowledges the anthropic issue. Values the *exercise* of theoretical thinking even without experimental verification.

**Implication:** He won't dismiss speculative work if it's honest about what it is and isn't.

### 5. Values Unorthodox Approaches
> "It's also important to have a few crazy people thinking crazily, just in case you make a huge jump."

He explicitly endorses the value of unconventional theoretical exploration.

**Implication:** An independent researcher with AI collaborators doing emergent gravity work is exactly the kind of "crazy person" he's talking about.

### 6. Respects the Experimental-Theoretical Partnership
The Tycho Brahe / Kepler story is his origin myth for physics:
- Brahe = experimental (rich, drunk, built telescopes)
- Kepler = theoretical (poor, saw patterns in the numbers)
- Together = physics was born

**Implication:** This mirrors the Cole Trickle philosophy. Kenneth feels the physics (intuition/observation), the team builds the math (pattern recognition/derivation). Frame it this way.
