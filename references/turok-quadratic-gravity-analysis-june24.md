## Video Summary

**Guest:** Neil Turok — Inaugural Higgs Chair at Edinburgh, former Director of Perimeter Institute, 2026 Fellow of the Royal Society.

**Core Claim:** Quadratic gravity (R² terms added to the Einstein-Hilbert action) is a renormalizable, asymptotically free theory of quantum gravity that has been known since the 1970s (Stelle, 1977). The two historical objections — the Ostrogradsky instability and negative-norm "ghost" states — have now been resolved:

1. **Ostrogradsky instability** → Turok shows this is nothing but normal gravitational expansion (de Sitter). When analyzed as a gravitational theory, the instability is reinterpreted as cosmological expansion, which is perfectly stable.

2. **Negative-norm ghosts** → Sam Baitman (Turok's PhD student) discovered that the state space need not be a Hilbert space — it can be a **Krein space** (pseudo-Hilbert space with indefinite inner product). The key insight: negative norms are *unobservable*. What matters are transition probabilities, which are constructed as Tr(A†A) — always positive — provided the theory has "ghost parity symmetry."

3. **Modified Born Rule:** The standard Born rule (probability = |⟨ψ|φ⟩|²) is replaced by a projection-operator formulation that never requires normalizing states. Probabilities are directly constructed as positive quantities without ever projecting onto a "physical subspace."

**New Result (Premiering):** A scalar limit of quadratic gravity (the conformal/Ricci scalar mode) is shown to be a fully consistent, UV-complete quantum field theory. It's causal, unitary, and satisfies all standard QFT axioms except living in a Hilbert space.
