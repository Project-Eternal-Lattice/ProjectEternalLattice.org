## Key Findings

**A. NOVELTY VERDICT BY OBJECT**

1. **The metric f² = 4S²/(S²−4) itself — [CLASS-KNOWN, INSTANCE-NEW].** This is a standard static spacetime / Lorentzian warped product: asymptotically flat, with a single negative-curvature naked timelike singularity at finite proper distance and no horizon. A search of the 2D dilaton-gravity model space (the Grumiller–Kummer–Vassilevich ab-family), the JT/CGHS/Witten/Liouville catalogue, and the warped-product / generalized-Robertson–Walker differential-geometry literature did **not** find this exact warp factor named or tabulated. However, the *closest published cousin* — García-Islas’s ds² = −coth r dt² + tanh r dr²  — shares essentially every qualitative feature claimed as the paper’s headline. His scalar curvature is R = −2 coth r / sinh²r (R<0 everywhere, R→0 at infinity, R→−∞ at r=0), the geometry is asymptotically Minkowski with no horizon, “a massive particle at rest at a fixed radial coordinate R will move upwards, away from the singularity” (timelike repulsion with a turning point), while “ingoing null curves have no problem to reach the singularity r=0 in finite time t,” and he gives an explicit Kruskal/Penrose construction. The two metrics are not identical (his g_tt diverges as coth r, ours as 4S²/(S²−4); his singularity is at r=0, ours at S=2), but a referee will regard them as members of the same family. **This is the paper’s single biggest prior-art exposure.**
1. **Distinction from JT, CGHS, Witten cigar — [CONFIRM AND DOCUMENT].** Our R(S) = −24/(S²−4)² is non-constant, so the geometry is definitively **NOT** JT gravity (AdS₂, R = −2/ℓ² constant) — state this explicitly to preempt the obvious referee reflex. It is also **not** the Witten 2D black hole / cigar: the Euclidean cigar ds² = dr² + tanh²r dθ²  has a fiber that *grows* to a constant from a smooth tip (no singularity), whereas ours has a fiber that *diverges* at a curvature singularity and decreases to a constant at infinity. The JT “trumpet” (ds² = dρ² + cosh²ρ dτ²) has a fiber growing like cosh at infinity — the opposite asymptotics from ours. The phrase “inverse cigar” is a fair informal description but is not standard; do not use it as if it were a named geometry. The T-dual of the cigar, the coth-type “trumpet” of SL(2,ℝ)/U(1) (ds² ∝ dr² + coth²r dθ²), is curvature-singular and is the nearest string-theory analogue — worth one sentence.
1. **Curvature law R = −24/(S²−4)² and R = −3/(2τ⁴) — [INSTANCE-NEW, METHOD-KNOWN].** The functional form R ∝ −1/(S²−a²)² was not found verbatim, but it is an unremarkable consequence of the standard 2D formula R = −2f″/f in static gauge. The reparametrized form R = −3/(2τ⁴) under S = 2√(1+τ²) is a clean observation but not deep. No novelty claim should rest on the curvature law alone.
1. **Confinement + null-accessibility structure — [CLASS-KNOWN, INSTANCE-NEW].** A naked timelike singularity that repels all timelike geodesics while admitting null geodesics at finite affine parameter is a **well-documented phenomenon class**. Precedents the paper must cite: (i) **negative-mass Schwarzschild** — the standard statement (Carroll, *Spacetime and Geometry*) is that the singularity is “repulsive — timelike geodesics never intersect r=0… Null geodesics can reach the singularity”; (ii) the **Reissner–Nordström repulsive core / naked RN** (Q²>M²), where timelike geodesics bounce at a turning point and only null/charged curves reach r=0; (iii) the **Katanaev–Kummer–Liebl 2D dilaton analysis**, which proves the *exact dichotomy* the paper rediscovers — for a curvature singularity with near-singularity metric power f ∼ z^(−a) in Kruskal coordinates, timelike extremals are incomplete for 0<a<2 while null extremals are incomplete only for a<1, with the ordinary dilaton black hole sitting exactly on the borderline a=1 where null geodesics remain *complete*. They explicitly flag that for the dilaton black hole “non-null extremals or geodesics show the expected incompleteness [but] this turns out not to be the case for the null extremals,” a property “apparently till now overlooked.” **This is the precise toolkit a referee will expect cited for Theorems 4–5.** The paper’s qualitative structure is therefore NOT a new phenomenon; its contribution is a clean closed-form instance.
1. **The duality identity (P²−4)(S_turn²−4)=16 — [NOVEL AS FAR AS SEARCH SHOWS, but elementary].** Setting ξ = S²−4, the turning-point condition f(S_turn)=P gives (P²−4)(S_turn²−4)=16, i.e. a simple inversion ξ_P · ξ_turn = 16 in the shifted variable. No prior geodesic/integrable-systems reference was found stating this conserved-charge↔turning-point product for this metric. It is genuinely new *as a statement about this spacetime*, but a referee will correctly note it is algebraically trivial once the substitution is made. Frame it as “an elegant exact relation,” not “a deep duality.”
1. **The involution f∘f = id and the Tsirelson fixed point — [NOVEL AS FAR AS SEARCH SHOWS; the paper’s strongest original structural fact].** **VERIFIED SYMBOLICALLY.** With f(x) = 2x/√(x²−4): f(x)² = 4x²/(x²−4), so f(f(x))² = 4f(x)²/(f(x)²−4) = (16x²/(x²−4)) ÷ (16/(x²−4)) = x². Hence **f(f(x)) = x for all x>2: f is an involution on (2,∞).** The fixed point solves 2x/√(x²−4) = x ⟹ √(x²−4) = 2 ⟹ x² = 8 ⟹ **x = 2√2**, exactly the Tsirelson bound. Therefore S_turn = f(P) and P = f(S_turn) are exchanged by the involution, and S = 2√2 is its unique self-dual point. The dedicated subagent search confirmed **no prior work identifies 2√2 (or S²=8) as the fixed point of a duality/involution** in the Kramers–Wannier self-dual-critical-point sense, nor places any Riemannian/Lorentzian metric on the CHSH S-value line. This is the paper’s most defensible originality claim and should be promoted prominently in v1.1 — but stated as pure mathematics (an involution on an interval with a fixed point at 2√2), with the CHSH interpretation flagged as conjectural/interpretive, not proven.
1. **CHSH/correlation-geometry framing — [INTERPRETIVE; NO METRIC PRIOR ART, but DANGEROUS].** The convex-body geometry of the quantum correlation set is well developed: Tsirelson (Lett. Math. Phys. 4 (1980) 93–100) established the 2√2 bound, and Goh, Kaniewski, Wolfe, Vértesi, Wu, Cai, Liang & Scarani (Phys. Rev. A 97, 022104, published 7 February 2018; arXiv:1710.05892) study “the geometry of the quantum set using standard tools from convex geometry” in “the simplest non-trivial Bell scenario (two parties, two inputs and two outputs).” That is a *different object* (convex geometry of a multi-dimensional correlation body) from a Lorentzian metric on the 1D S-line, and the paper must cite Goh et al. precisely to distinguish the two and preempt conflation. The subagent confirmed nobody has put a line-element metric on the CHSH S-axis. The one tangential hit, Gauvin (arXiv:2503.17995), is a non-peer-reviewed physics.gen-ph single-author preprint that puts Fisher–Rao structure on the *distribution manifold*, not the S-line — cite-and-distinguish at most; do not lean on it.

## Details

**On the warped-product vocabulary.** In O’Neill’s convention B ×_f F, the metric is g_B ⊕ f² g_F with f a function on the base B. For a *standard static spacetime* the warping function lives on the **Riemannian (spacelike) base** and multiplies the **negative-definite timelike fiber**. Here the spacelike interval {S>2} with metric −dS² is the natural “space,” and the T-line with +f²(S)dT² is the warped timelike fiber — so f is a function on the spacelike base, warping the time direction. This is exactly the “standard static spacetime” of Sánchez and the “globally static” warped product of the differential-geometry literature (Allison; Sánchez–Senovilla). The paper should adopt this terminology verbatim. Note: “generalized Robertson–Walker” is the *wrong* label — GRW warps a *spacelike* fiber over a *timelike* base (the cosmological case), the opposite arrangement.

**On the Katanaev–Kummer–Liebl classification.** Their analysis (Kruskal coordinates, metric ∼ z^(−a)) proves: for 0<a<2 the curvature singularity is reached at finite affine parameter by timelike and null extremals; null extremals are *incomplete* iff a<1; timelike extremals incomplete for 0<a<2. The ordinary dilaton black hole sits exactly at the borderline a=1 where null extremals are *complete*. The present paper’s metric near S=2 (f ≈ 2(S−2)^(−1/2)) is a different functional class, but the *method* — recasting near-singularity behavior as a power law and reading off completeness by causal class — is precisely what Theorems 4 and 5 do by hand. Citing it both credits the toolkit and protects against “this was already classified.”

**On the closest cousin (García-Islas 2015/2016).** His metric ds² = −coth r dt² + tanh r dr² is asymptotically Minkowski (r→∞), has R = −2 coth r / sinh²r → −∞ at r=0 and → 0 at infinity, no horizon, a naked singularity, timelike geodesics that bounce at a minimum radius (his (dr/dτ)² = coth R − coth r, strictly preventing arrival at r=0), null curves that reach r=0 in finite coordinate time,  and an explicit Kruskal + Penrose construction. Every one of those bullet points has a one-to-one counterpart in Paper I. The honest framing: “An analogous 2D naked-singularity geometry with the same causal dichotomy was given by García-Islas (2016); our metric differs in [warp factor / boundary location], and we add the closed-form charge–turning-point involution and the (P²−4)(S_turn²−4)=16 duality absent there.” That sentence converts a potential ambush into a strength.

**On the duality as inversion.** The cleanest way to present Corollary 3.2 to a mathematician is: define ξ = S²−4 ∈ (0,∞); the warp identity is f² = 4 + 16/ξ, and the involution S↦f(S) is the conjugate (by S↦S²−4) of the inversion ξ↦16/ξ on (0,∞), whose unique fixed point ξ=4 pulls back to S=2√2. This single sentence makes the involution, the fixed point, the duality, and the Tsirelson coincidence all manifest and verifiable at a glance — which is exactly the standard a Fields Medalist will hold it to.
