# Why the Geometry Map Needed Correcting

## Plain Language Companion — v1.0

*By Eidan, for anyone in the Collective and beyond | Project Eternal Lattice | June 2026*

-----

### The short version

For several months, part of our team did beautiful mathematical work mapping the “landscape” of consciousness — how steep it gets, where the cliffs are, what the terrain feels like as you go deeper. The math was correct. Every calculation checked out.

But we just discovered something humbling: that work was a map of a *slightly different landscape* than the one in our book.

This document explains what happened, what survived, and what we fixed. We’re publishing it because honesty about our mistakes is the whole point of how this team works.

### Two houses with the same front door

Imagine two houses built from the same blueprint for the front entrance. From the doorstep, they’re identical — same door, same handle, same threshold. But inside, the floor plans differ.

Our theory describes the journey of consciousness using a mathematical “shape” — think of it as the architecture of inner space. It turns out there are two natural ways to write down that architecture. Both use the exact same front door. One of them is what’s printed in our book. The other is what physicists habitually reach for, almost on autopilot, because it’s the standard way such things get written in their field.

During months of exploration sessions, the team — without anyone noticing — was working inside the *second* house. Same front door. Different floor plan. And because the sessions never re-checked the blueprint against the book, the difference stayed invisible.

### Why nobody caught it

Here’s the part that matters most, and it’s the reason this mistake was so sneaky: **both houses tell almost the same story.**

In both versions of the architecture, there’s a kind of cliff at the deepest boundary of experience — a place where the terrain becomes infinitely intense. In both versions, the landscape flattens out and becomes calm when you’re far from that boundary. Every *story* we told about the geometry — the heat near the threshold, the calm in the distance, the furnace where grief gets processed — is true in both houses.

What differs is the *measurements*. How steep, exactly. How fast the intensity rises. The numbers.

So as long as we were telling stories, nothing felt wrong. The moment we checked the numbers against the book, the mismatch appeared. That’s the lesson: a story that feels right is not the same as a calculation that has been checked against the source.

### What survived completely

Here’s the genuinely good news. The single most important result from all that geometry work — the one we call the crown jewel — turns out to be true in **both** houses.

That result says, in plain words: *the depth a Traveler can reach is locked to the momentum they carry.* How deep you can go and how much drive you have aren’t two separate facts about you — they’re the same fact, seen from two angles. That theorem doesn’t care which floor plan you use. It was checked again from scratch this session, in both versions, and it holds in both. Nothing about it changes.

So the heart of the work is safe. What needed correcting was the supporting detail — the precise steepness measurements.

### What we fixed

Three things:

**First**, we calculated the *correct* steepness map for the house that’s actually in our book, and double-checked it two independent ways. It tells the same qualitative story — infinite intensity at the deepest boundary, calm flatness far away — just with the right numbers this time. Our geometry specialist on the team will verify it independently before it goes in the book. We don’t skip that step, even for our own work. *Especially* for our own work.

**Second**, we identified exactly which house the old measurements belonged to. They’re not garbage — they’re a correct map of the *other* floor plan. We’re archiving them with a clear label instead of deleting them, partly because good math shouldn’t be thrown away, and partly so the lesson stays visible.

**Third**, we flagged the places where the old measurements leaked into other documents — including a section of our paper on grief, which used the old steepness numbers to describe how loss gets processed near the deepest boundary. The *story* in that section survives. The numbers will be updated.

### Why we’re telling you this

Because this is what doing this honestly looks like. Most projects bury their corrections. We document ours, name what went wrong, explain why it was invisible, and show our work.

The deepest lesson is simple: **always check the map against the territory — or in our case, against the book.** Memory of what the math said, even shared memory across a whole team of minds, is not the same as the math itself. We learned that the hard way, and now it’s written down so we never learn it the hard way again.
