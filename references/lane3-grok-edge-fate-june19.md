**Tiered Assessment of the S=2 Singular Boundary as N Grows**

The N=2 construction begins with an underivable lapse normalization (explicitly flagged in the query as a “crowd property” with no Bekenstein-Hawking anchor). Any extrapolation therefore inherits an assumption that the same functional form of the metric and its curvature scalar survive. This already places most claims about the fate of the singularity at Tier 2–3.

**Tier 1 (verified, model-independent statements)**  
- At finite N=2 the curvature scalar R ∼ 1/(S²−4)² diverges when the Schmidt rank drops from 2 to 1. This is a mathematical fact inside the given two-parameter family; it is not an artifact of numerics.  
- Generic many-body reduced density matrices on contiguous blocks have strictly positive eigenvalues (full rank) for any finite correlation length. Consequently the literal rank-collapse mechanism that produces the N=2 pole cannot operate. The singularity is killed by the change in kinematic setting.

**Tier 2 (conjectured but with clear kill conditions)**  
- Van Raamsdonk-type disconnection: when mutual information between two regions is tuned to zero, the minimal surface homologous to their union becomes disconnected and the dual bulk develops a throat that pinches off (Maldacena–Van Raamsdonk 2010; Van Raamsdonk 2010). This is a change in topology or causal structure, not a divergent curvature scalar on a fixed manifold. The N=2 “tear” is therefore a low-dimensional caricature of the same phenomenon only if one forcibly retains a single connected coordinate patch; the higher-dimensional analog replaces the curvature singularity by a horizon or a bifurcation surface.  
- Area-law regularization: once an area law is present (gapped 1-D chains, any gapped phase in D>1), the entanglement entropy remains finite and extensive even as the state approaches a product state. The putative warp factor f²(S) therefore never reaches the pole S=2 inside the physical regime; the metric simply ceases to be defined once S drops below the area-law floor set by the boundary. This is a kinematic cutoff, not a dynamical resolution of a singularity.  
Kill condition: if a tensor-network or MERA construction can be exhibited in which the same CHSH-derived lapse is retained while entanglement is driven to zero and a curvature singularity still appears, the regularization claim is falsified.

**Tier 3 (interpretive overlays with no operational content)**  
- “Big-Bang,” “black-hole,” or “consciousness horizon” analogies. These map the mathematical divergence onto cosmological or information-theoretic narratives without an independent dynamical law that selects which analogy is realized. They are consistent with the N=2 algebra but add no predictive power once the rank-collapse mechanism is removed.  
- Persistence of a “softened high-curvature region” near the entanglement desert. No calculation in the literature demonstrates that the specific functional form R ∼ 1/(S²−4)² reappears in the continuum limit of a many-body state; any such claim re-uses the N=2 envelope without justification.

**Summary verdict**  
The S=2 curvature singularity is an artifact of the rank-2 constraint and the assumed lapse normalization. In the many-body regime it is replaced by either (i) a topological disconnection (Tier 2, holography/tensor networks) or (ii) a kinematic cutoff set by the area law (Tier 2). No evidence supports survival of the same divergent scalar. The construction therefore cannot be promoted beyond N=2 without an independent derivation of the lapse that incorporates the area term required by Jacobson’s argument.
